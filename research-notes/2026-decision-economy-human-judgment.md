## Source Context

Tamrat Y argues that artificial intelligence changes the world not because it thinks like humans, but because it can help humans think better. The associated discussion asks how AI can elevate judgment in unpredictable domains such as geopolitics and ethics rather than merely accelerate inherited habits.

## Derived Governance Distinction

```text
Faster reasoning does not guarantee better judgment.
Speed amplifies the structure through which decisions are made.
```

A weak decision structure becomes faster fragility when accelerated. In uncertain or adversarial environments, the system must do more than answer who, what, when, where, why, and how questions quickly. It must also detect when:

- material inputs are incomplete;
- evidence is adversarial or manipulated;
- the governing frame no longer fits reality;
- constraints are being optimized away;
- an individually rational action becomes harmful as it propagates;
- recoverability or legitimacy degrades during execution.

## Admissibility Question

The relevant governance question is not only whether a proposed decision appears intelligent at selection time. It is whether the decision remains admissible as it propagates through a changing system.

```text
Decision quality at selection time != admissibility throughout execution
```

## Human Judgment Responsibilities

Human judgment remains responsible for:

1. recognizing frame failure;
2. identifying uncertainty that cannot safely be collapsed into a single answer;
3. preserving non-optimizable ethical, legal, and institutional constraints;
4. distinguishing prediction confidence from execution authority;
5. maintaining recoverability when system conditions or operator authority degrade;
6. stopping or revising execution when legitimacy no longer persists.
