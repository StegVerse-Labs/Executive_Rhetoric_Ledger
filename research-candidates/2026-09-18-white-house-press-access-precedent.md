## Research question

Reconstruct the September 18, 2026 White House announcement affecting CNN, MS NOW, and Politico and determine, from source-bounded evidence, exactly what access is restricted, what governmental rationale is stated, how implementation differs across White House access surfaces, how existing First Amendment and press-access precedent applies, and what rule could survive for use by future administrations against ideologically different outlets.

## Required distinctions

- criticism of press coverage vs governmental access restriction;
- hard pass / routine press-facility access vs event-specific invitation;
- White House briefing-room access vs pool participation;
- East Room or other opened press facilities vs Oval Office, Air Force One, and other restricted presidential workspaces;
- announcement vs implemented credential/access action;
- preliminary injunction or stay posture vs final merits judgment;
- viewpoint-discrimination allegation/finding vs final constitutional disposition;
- current-administration facts vs a general institutional rule usable by later administrations.

## Initial evidence state

Contemporaneous Reuters and Associated Press reporting locate the September 18 announcement and report that the President linked the exclusions to what he characterized as false or objectionable coverage. Direct first-party custody of the exact presidential/White House statement remains required before treating the wording as primary executive evidence.

Primary legal anchors include the First Amendment, `Sherrill v. Knight`, the April 8, 2025 district-court preliminary-injunction order in `Associated Press v. Budowich`, and the June 6, 2025 D.C. Circuit stay order. Those authorities must be retained by procedural posture and access-surface scope rather than collapsed into one categorical rule.

## Research posture

This candidate does not endorse or oppose any administration, outlet, party, or press-access policy. It does not make a motive, final constitutional, legal-liability, policy-quality, or partisan finding. Cross-administration analysis must apply the same institutional rule whether a Republican, Democratic, or other administration is acting and regardless of the ideological identity of the affected outlet.
