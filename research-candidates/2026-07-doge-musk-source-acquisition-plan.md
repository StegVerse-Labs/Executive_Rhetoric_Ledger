## Required secondary and oversight records

- GAO fraud-loss methodology and limitations;
- DOL Inspector General pandemic unemployment-insurance fraud estimates and recovery posture;
- congressional hearing records and written questions concerning DOGE authority and conflicts;
- recognized wire-service reporting used only as a locator and contradiction layer until primary records are obtained;
- watchdog or expert analyses with disclosed methodology and conflicts.

## Control comparisons

1. Prior SGEs or executive officials whose private companies faced active federal enforcement.
2. Enforcement matters paused or dismissed during broad administration changes where the beneficiary had no government role.
3. Agency workforce reductions affecting unrelated regulated entities.
4. Inspector-general removals not involving investigations of the official directing or benefiting from removal.
5. Budget reductions that reallocated rather than reduced aggregate spending under prior administrations.

## Required receipt posture

Each collected object must receive a Source Posture receipt recording:

- exact title and URL;
- archive or immutable copy location;
- publication and access dates;
- institutional proximity;
- evidence role;
- verification status;
- admissibility use;
- confidence and red flags;
- which narrative claims it supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved.

## Stop conditions

The assessment may not be promoted until:

- the § 208 waiver question is resolved or documented as unavailable after a reproducible search;
- every agency-matter row has a current docket or official-status source;
- budget figures distinguish proposed, enacted, obligated, and expended amounts;
- DOGE claimed savings distinguish announced, cancelled, duplicated, corrected, and realized values;
- a control comparison tests whether the observed cluster is exceptional;
- an independent reviewer confirms the boundary between chronology, inference, and legal conclusion.
