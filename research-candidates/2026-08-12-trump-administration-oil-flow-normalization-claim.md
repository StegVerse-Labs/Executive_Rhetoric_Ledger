## Research question

Did Trump-administration statements that Middle East oil and natural-gas flows had returned to approximately pre-crisis or normal levels accurately describe the underlying physical energy system, or did the statements conflate one or more distinct layers such as production, export loadings, inventory drawdowns, pipeline bypasses, tanker movements, and Strait of Hormuz transits?

## Candidate claim under examination

Reported administration position:

- President Donald Trump said there was substantial oil again moving out through the Strait of Hormuz.
- Energy Secretary Chris Wright reportedly characterized oil and natural-gas movement as being at or near pre-crisis levels and expected flows to exceed that level.
- Trump also reportedly asserted that a recent day represented a record level of oil movement through the strait.

These are **candidate public claims** pending capture of authoritative video, transcript, White House/DOE release, or other primary record containing the exact language, date, and context.

## Counter-evidence lead

A contemporaneous Gulf News report, summarizing tanker analytics attributed to Kpler, reported 71 confirmed Strait of Hormuz transits over the referenced weekend, with a peak of 35 crossings on Saturday, and stated that the administration's characterization of pre-crisis flow levels was not supported by those observations.

This is a secondary-source lead only. The underlying Kpler dataset, methodology, vessel classification, cargo estimates, and pre-crisis comparison window must be acquired before a finding is authorized.

## Analytical boundary

Do **not** treat any single measure as equivalent to “oil flowing normally.” Reconstruct the physical chain separately:

1. **Production** — barrels produced at fields and processing facilities.
2. **Available export supply** — production plus/minus storage changes and domestic refinery demand.
3. **Terminal loadings** — barrels physically loaded at Gulf export terminals.
4. **Bypass pipelines** — volumes moved through routes that avoid Hormuz.
5. **Tanker transits** — number and capacity of vessels crossing Hormuz, including uncertainty from AIS-dark or partially observed traffic.
6. **Delivered exports** — barrels actually reaching destination markets.
7. **Natural-gas/LNG flows** — separately measured; not interchangeable with crude-oil flows.

A recovery at one layer does not prove normalization at the others. Inventory drawdowns, previously loaded cargoes, pipeline rerouting, AIS-dark movements, or changing vessel sizes could make one observable measure rise while production or total delivered supply remains below baseline.

## Required baseline

Define “normal” before evaluating the claim. Candidate baselines include:

- 30-day pre-war average;
- same-season prior-year average;
- official EIA historical Hormuz throughput;
- Kpler/Vortexa/MarineTraffic vessel and cargo estimates;
- exporting-country official production and terminal-loading data.

The evaluation must state the denominator, date window, unit, and whether the comparison concerns vessel count, cargo capacity, barrels/day, LNG volume, production, exports, or delivered supply.

## Evaluation tests

### Test A — literal throughput

Was measured petroleum throughput through Hormuz at or near the selected pre-crisis barrels/day baseline on the date(s) claimed?

### Test B — vessel-count proxy

If the administration relied on vessel counts, did those counts represent comparable vessel classes and cargo capacities, and were they sufficient to infer normalized barrels/day?

### Test C — broader export recovery

Were total Middle Eastern petroleum exports near normal after including bypass pipelines and inventory drawdowns, even if Hormuz transit itself remained impaired?

### Test D — production recovery

Had underlying regional production returned to baseline, or were exports being supported by stored inventory and routing workarounds?

### Test E — natural-gas claim

Were gas/LNG flows independently near baseline, and was the same definition of “normal” applied?

### Test F — statement precision

Did the original speaker explicitly refer to Hormuz transit, regional exports, production, global supply, or a broader energy-flow metric? A finding must track the speaker's actual scope rather than a headline paraphrase.

## Candidate discrepancy pattern

The present lead supports investigation of a possible **metric-substitution / denominator ambiguity** pattern:

> A public statement may characterize a system as “normal” by referencing a favorable observable subset while other physically necessary layers remain materially impaired.

This pattern is **not yet a finding**. It becomes assessable only after the exact statement, baseline, and independent physical-flow evidence are preserved.
