## Non-equivalent interpretations to preserve

### Interpretation A — direct private knowledge

Clinton personally observed, heard from, or communicated with multiple Republican officials who welcomed Canadian resistance to Trump.

### Interpretation B — informed elite inference

Clinton inferred private Republican sentiment from longstanding relationships, public dissent, constituent exposure, votes, and familiar party behavior without asserting a literal witnessed event.

### Interpretation C — rhetorical population compression

Clinton used “Republicans” or “the GOP” to generalize from a smaller observable or privately known faction.

### Interpretation D — headline expansion

The originating article or MSN headline broadened a more qualified Clinton statement into a GOP-wide characterization.

### Interpretation E — two distinct approval populations

One Republican faction supports the tariff confrontation and may treat retaliation as expected pain or justification for escalation, while a different faction privately welcomes Canada imposing resistance that it will not or cannot impose through Congress.

These interpretations may coexist. None is authorized as the terminal finding at candidate intake.

## Disconfirming and qualifying evidence

The political-atmosphere interpretation would weaken if:

- Clinton explicitly identified the remark as humor or unsupported speculation;
- the headline converted a singular or narrowly bounded reference into a GOP-wide claim;
- named Republicans denied the described reaction and independent evidence supported those denials;
- subsequent records showed the relevant Republican faction consistently and actively opposed Canadian resistance rather than privately welcoming it.

The signal would strengthen if:

- Clinton identified direct observations or contemporaneous private conversations;
- Republican officials later acknowledged welcoming Canadian resistance;
- contemporaneous messages, recordings, memoirs, or sourced reporting independently corroborated the described private sentiment;
- observable votes and conduct aligned with the bounded population she actually described.
