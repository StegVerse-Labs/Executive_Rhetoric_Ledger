## Candidate proposition

Mark Cuban has publicly advocated broad employee equity participation and a higher corporate tax burden for companies that do not distribute equity broadly, framing ownership participation as a mechanism for reducing wealth concentration.

The ERL economic-distribution lane uses this candidate to distinguish wages from capital participation and to ask who captures enterprise value appreciation.

## Measurement relationship

The relevant analytical chain is:

```text
Production / Productivity
  -> Producer Surplus / Margin Capacity
  -> Distribution of Created Value
  -> Household Economic Position
```

`Producer Margin` remains separately measurable from `Distribution`. High productivity, margins, or valuation do not establish broad household value capture.

Ownership Distribution / Capital Participation should separately measure, where evidence permits:

- employee equity participation rate;
- employee equity value as a share of compensation;
- breadth of participation across wage bands;
- employee appreciation capture;
- executive/founder appreciation capture;
- external-shareholder appreciation capture;
- retirement-account exposure;
- dilution and vesting effects;
- realized versus unrealized gains;
- ownership concentration before and after value-creation events;
- treatment of contractors, franchise workers, temporary workers, and other non-equity labor.

## Required evidence before efficacy promotion

- exact primary/public Cuban statements;
- historical employee-equity practices at Broadcast.com and other Cuban-controlled companies;
- independently verifiable worker outcome data;
- comparison with wage-only firms;
- tax-incidence analysis;
- dilution, vesting, and liquidity constraints;
- distributional effects across income bands;
- survivorship-selection controls;
- acquisition/IPO-event controls;
- counterexamples where broad equity participation did not materially improve household wealth.

## Non-claims

This candidate does not establish that Cuban's proposed tax/equity mechanism is optimal, effective, equitable in every implementation, or preferable to tax-and-transfer, wealth-tax, ESOP, cooperative, wage-only, or other distribution models.
