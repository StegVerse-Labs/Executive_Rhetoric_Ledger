## Research purpose

Reconstruct the September 2026 NATO/Russia readiness signal proposition-by-proposition and distinguish:

1. readiness planning from an imminent-threat assessment;
2. limited/probing attack scenarios from general conventional invasion;
3. hybrid, cyber, sabotage, drone, infrastructure, and territorial-incursion scenarios;
4. official U.S. statements from NATO-wide assessments;
5. intelligence reporting from public policy recommendations;
6. attribution confidence from response authority;
7. Article 4 consultation, Article 5 collective-defense thresholds, and actions below those thresholds;
8. European defense-burden shifts from direct evidence of Russian attack intent.

## Governing distinctions

```text
prepare_for_attack != attack_is_imminent
limited_probe != general_invasion
hybrid_operation != automatically_armed_attack
official_attribution != independently_proven_state_control
article_4_consultation != article_5_trigger
article_5_trigger != predetermined_response
readiness_increase != escalation_decision
public_warning != disclosed_intelligence_basis
```

## Initial analytical hypothesis set

These are research hypotheses only:

- H1: NATO is moving from generic deterrence planning toward explicit preparation for limited Russian probes.
- H2: public warnings are primarily intended to increase readiness and deterrence rather than signal a current imminent-attack assessment.
- H3: the highest near-term decision risk is an ambiguous, attributable-but-contestable operation below the scale of general invasion.
- H4: distributed allied decision authority makes evidence provenance, attribution confidence, proportionality, and shared state reconstruction central to crisis response.
- H5: European defense-burden shifts and U.S. force-posture changes may materially alter deterrence calculations without themselves proving increased Russian attack intent.

No hypothesis is promoted by this candidate.

## Promotion boundary

- Russia is about to attack NATO;
- Russia has decided to attack NATO;
- any reported sabotage or drone incident was state-directed unless proposition-level evidence supports it;
- any particular event satisfies Article 5;
- NATO has selected a military response;
- U.S. calls for readiness establish a secret intelligence conclusion;
- StegVerse would have prevented or resolved a NATO crisis.

Promotion requires primary-source custody, chronology reconstruction, contradiction review, proposition-relative attribution, authority mapping, and independent review.
