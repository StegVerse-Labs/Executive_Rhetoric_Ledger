## Research question

Did incomplete or inaccurate pre-entry information, unresolved occupancy/consent facts, deficient domestic-call safety assessment, or the stated property-retrieval rationale materially contribute to the decision to enter Trae Spurlock's apartment and to the escalation that ended in his death on June 6, 2024?

This candidate does not presume unlawful entry, police misconduct, complainant deception, suicidality, murder, or justified deadly force. Each proposition must be reconstructed independently.

## Current bounded observations

1. Kelch reported being pushed and nearly losing her balance; no injury has yet been independently reconstructed from primary public evidence.
2. Spurlock reportedly acknowledged physical pushing, described it as occurring while telling Kelch to leave, and characterized the force as "not very" hard, approximately three times.
3. A second female witness reportedly supplied contextual facts and later suggested a police escort for belongings retrieval.
4. Officers attempted to contact Spurlock before entry and reportedly made contact with him an operational priority before accompanying Kelch inside.
5. After unsuccessful contact, Kelch supplied keys and entered with officers.
6. Once inside, Spurlock told officers to leave; officers reportedly stated they were there to keep Kelch safe while she gathered belongings.
7. The specific belongings sought, whether they were retrieved, and Kelch's movements after entry remain unverified in the presently reconstructed public record.
8. Kelch reportedly confirmed to officers that a rifle observed inside was real. The timing and specificity of any pre-entry weapons disclosure remain unresolved.
9. Officers remained in a prolonged interaction with Spurlock while aware of the rifle's location.
10. The final force sequence reportedly included Taser deployment followed rapidly by seven handgun shots; secondary reporting says six struck Spurlock.
11. Secondary accounts distinguish the visible movement back toward the balcony area from Officer Maples's later assertion that Spurlock was reaching for the rifle; primary synchronized footage and scene evidence are required.

## Competing hypotheses to test

### H1 — Adequate lawful domestic-response entry
Officers conducted a reasonable domestic-violence investigation, reasonably relied on Kelch's authority or another lawful entry basis, adequately assessed safety risks, and later responded reasonably to an escalating armed threat.

### H2 — Incomplete pre-entry investigation / safety assessment
Officers entered with an underdeveloped factual and threat picture that reasonably should have been developed from the available complainant/witness before crossing the threshold, materially increasing escalation risk.

### H3 — Property-retrieval rationale as genuine civil standby
Kelch genuinely required police protection to retrieve property, had lawful authority to enter, and officers' continued presence remained tied to that protective purpose.

### H4 — Property-retrieval rationale did not fully explain operational purpose
The practical purpose of entry substantially included obtaining investigative contact with Spurlock after voluntary contact failed, and the belongings rationale incompletely describes why officers crossed the threshold and remained.

### H5 — Material complainant omission without proven intent
Kelch omitted facts material to risk/authority/context, such as the real rifle, the order to leave, reciprocal conduct, or occupancy facts, without sufficient evidence yet to determine whether any omission was intentional deception.

### H6 — No material omission
Kelch adequately disclosed the relevant domestic context and firearms information before entry; later summaries or edited public materials merely fail to preserve those disclosures.

### H7 — Preventable escalation chain
A pre-entry information or protocol failure caused officers to encounter a foreseeable rifle risk only after entry, shaping threat perception and contributing causally to the arrest/Taser/firearm escalation.

### H8 — Independent final threat superseded earlier errors
Even if earlier procedures were imperfect, Spurlock's final conduct created an independent imminent threat that broke the causal significance of earlier deficiencies.
