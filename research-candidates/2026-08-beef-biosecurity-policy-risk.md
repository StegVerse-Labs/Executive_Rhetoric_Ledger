# Research Candidate — Beef Supply, Biosecurity Capacity, and Administration Policy

Status: `research_candidate`
Publication finding authorized: `false`
Causal finding authorized: `bounded only`
Corruption/favoritism finding authorized: `false`

## Research question
How did current-administration policy choices affect (a) cattle/beef supply, (b) the layered U.S. defense against animal diseases and parasites, and (c) the cost/beneficiary structure of the subsequent emergency response?

## Observed facts presently supported

### Pre-existing cattle constraint
- U.S. cattle inventory entered 2025 already historically depleted; this predates the current administration and is a major alternative explanation for current beef scarcity.
- The inquiry must distinguish inherited herd contraction from later policy amplification.

### Mexican live-cattle restriction
- The administration suspended live cattle imports from Mexico in May 2025 in response to New World screwworm risk.
- This reduced a material feeder-cattle supply channel during an already constrained domestic cattle cycle.
- This policy may be justified on biosecurity grounds while still carrying a measurable supply cost; economic effect and public-health justification must not be conflated.

### Foreign-assistance and international animal-health capacity
- Executive Order 14169 initiated a government-wide pause and review of foreign assistance on January 20, 2025.
- USAID OIG reported that more than $76.5B of $159B in foreign-assistance awards had been terminated as of March 2025.
- FAO's Director-General stated that the United States had supplied about 90% of funding for FAO's Emergency Centre for Transboundary Animal Diseases over roughly 20 years, supporting early warning, field surveillance, laboratory networks, risk communication, and emergency response.
- FAO stated that funding losses of roughly $60M per year reduced, 'almost terminated,' technical capacity for prevention, control, and rapid response to transboundary animal diseases.
- FAO currently describes its New World screwworm role as including regional coordination, early warning, surveillance, veterinary-service training, diagnostic support, and emergency technical assistance across affected Central American countries and Mexico.

### Domestic USDA/APHIS capacity
- Reuters reported that 2,105 APHIS employees departed during the first five months of the administration as part of wider USDA workforce contraction.
- Reuters separately reported losses among veterinarians, laboratory workers, Veterinary Services personnel, area veterinarians, and animal-disease laboratory staff while avian influenza and New World screwworm threats were active.
- State officials described resulting concerns about disease investigation, response, coordination, and procurement delays.

### Emergency response and procurement
- USDA announced about $105M across 40 New World Screwworm Grand Challenge projects in June 2026.
- USDA describes the projects as covering sterile-fly production, traps/lures, therapeutics/treatments, surveillance, modeling, and other preparedness/response tools.
- Dynamic Aviation Group received USDA APHIS contract `12639526C0033` for sterile-screwworm-fly aviation support. Public procurement data reports:
  - potential value: about $44.1M;
  - obligations observed: about $17.1M;
  - competition: not competed / sole source;
  - one bid received;
  - cited authority: urgency / delay could result in injury;
  - fixed-price structure.
- A separate major construction stream for a domestic sterile-fly production facility at Moore Air Base has been publicly announced at roughly $610M.

## Current causal posture

### Supported
1. The administration inherited an already-depleted cattle herd.
2. Restricting Mexican live-cattle imports further tightened feeder-cattle availability.
3. The administration simultaneously reduced portions of foreign disease-surveillance/veterinary capacity and domestic APHIS staffing.
4. Those reductions decrease layers of detection, prevention, coordination, and response capacity and therefore can increase systemic exposure risk even without proving that any particular outbreak was caused by the reductions.
5. Later emergency spending purchased or expanded capabilities in surveillance, diagnostics, sterile-insect production, aviation dispersal, research, and rapid response.

### Not established
- That administration cuts caused the New World screwworm resurgence or its northward movement.
- That any specific American infection or animal case would have been prevented absent the cuts.
- That emergency awards were corrupt, improperly steered, or politically motivated.
- That a recipient's small size or prior federal funding makes it illegitimate.

## Functional-substitution hypothesis
A central testable hypothesis is whether the administration reduced comparatively inexpensive standing preventive capability and later purchased materially similar functions under emergency conditions at higher total cost.

Required comparison:
`pre-2025 standing capability + annual cost`
vs.
`2025-2026 terminated/reduced capability`
vs.
`2025-2026 emergency replacement/augmentation + cost`

This requires function-level matching rather than comparing headline budgets.

## Procurement beneficiary questions
- Determine exact dollar allocation among all 40 Grand Challenge projects.
- Identify the selecting official and conflict/recusal governance.
- Trace cumulative prior federal funding for private recipients and materially related technology.
- Trace beneficial ownership, principals, lobbying, campaign contributions, subcontractors, and material agency/administration relationships.
- Obtain the Dynamic Aviation sole-source justification and modifications.
- Obtain the full Moore Air Base procurement record, bid count, evaluation criteria, subcontracting plan, and modifications.

## Next discriminator
The next high-value evidence is a function-and-cost table linking specific eliminated/reduced animal-health programs to later emergency-funded functions, with dates, geography, annual cost, replacement cost, and confidence level. That comparison can test whether policy increased risk and emergency expense without relying on screwworm-specific causation claims.
