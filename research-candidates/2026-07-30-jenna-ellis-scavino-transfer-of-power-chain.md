## Question presented

How much evidentiary validity should be assigned to Jenna Ellis's statement to Fulton County prosecutors that White House Deputy Chief of Staff Dan Scavino told her, after the 2020 election, that President Donald Trump was not going to leave office and that the group would remain in power?

The ledger separates five propositions that are frequently collapsed in public retellings:

1. an authentic Fulton County proffer interview occurred;
2. Ellis made the recorded statement;
3. Scavino made the statement to Ellis substantially as recalled;
4. Scavino accurately represented Trump's actual intention;
5. Trump possessed or authorized an operational plan to remain in power unlawfully.

Evidence supporting one proposition does not automatically establish the next.

## Attribution chain

```text
Trump's actual state of mind or instruction
-> Scavino's access to and interpretation of Trump
-> Scavino's alleged statement at a White House Christmas party
-> Ellis's perception and later memory
-> Ellis's October 23, 2023 proffer statement
-> leaked excerpts supplied in criminal discovery
-> ABC News / Washington Post publication
-> social-media caption and recirculation
```

## Chain-node assessment

### Node A — Authenticity of the proffer recording

**Current validity: very high.**

ABC News published video excerpts identifying the session as Ellis's October 23, 2023 confidential proffer with Fulton County investigators. The following day, Ellis entered her guilty plea. After publication, Fulton County prosecutors sought a protective order concerning confidential discovery videos, and the court addressed the disclosure. These events strongly authenticate the recording as criminal-case discovery rather than fabricated media.

Remaining requirement: obtain the complete original proffer object, file metadata, discovery production identifier, and complete transcript from the lawful custodian.

### Node B — Whether Ellis made the statement

**Current validity: very high.**

The video directly records Ellis saying that Scavino told her in an excited tone that "the boss is not going to leave under any circumstances" and "we are just going to stay in power." This node does not depend on a reporter's paraphrase.

Remaining requirement: confirm that the public excerpt is continuous and unaltered through the complete proffer recording and transcript.

### Node C — Whether Scavino made the statement to Ellis

**Current validity: moderate.**

This proposition rests primarily on Ellis's recollection nearly three years later. Her account contains details that increase testability: an identified speaker, setting, approximate date, conversational trigger, tone, reciprocal dialogue, and acknowledgment that both had consumed alcohol.

The account is weakened by the absence of a contemporaneous recording, message, diary entry, third-party witness, or sworn Scavino response. Ellis also had cooperation incentives and had just agreed to plead guilty to aiding and abetting false statements. Her prior propagation of unverified election claims requires independent corroboration rather than categorical acceptance.

No located public source contained a substantive Scavino denial. Nonresponse is not corroboration and must not be treated as an admission.

### Node D — Whether Scavino accurately conveyed Trump's intention

**Current validity: moderate, contextually strengthened but not directly verified.**

Scavino was not a remote commentator. Congressional records describe him as a long-time Trump employee, White House deputy chief of staff for communications, participant in post-election activity, frequent collaborator on Trump's social-media language, and a person present with or in repeated contact with Trump at critical times. That proximity materially increases his capacity to know Trump's position.

The alleged statement is also consistent with public and insider evidence that Trump continued denying the certified result, publicly urged continued resistance, pressured officials and the vice president, and resisted acknowledging that the election was over. Contextual consistency increases plausibility but does not prove that Scavino was transmitting a direct instruction rather than boasting, exaggerating, joking, or expressing his own expectation.

### Node E — Whether Trump authorized an unlawful operational plan to remain in power

**Current validity from this chain alone: low.**

Ellis did not report hearing the statement directly from Trump. The clip does not identify a specific operational directive, personnel assignment, use of force, continuity instrument, or physical plan to remain inside the White House. Other records may establish separate efforts to reverse or delay the certified result, but those must be linked independently rather than inferred solely from this conversation.

Trump ultimately departed the White House on January 20, 2021. That fact decreases any literal claim that he executed a plan never to leave the building, but it does not resolve what he intended, considered, attempted, or communicated on December 19, 2020.

## Evidence increasing validity

### 1. Direct audiovisual capture of Ellis's account

The public object records Ellis's own words, demeanor, qualifications, and description. It is stronger than an anonymous quotation or reporter-only summary.

### 2. Criminal-proffer setting and truthfulness obligation

Ellis made the statement in a cooperation session linked to a plea agreement. A knowingly false account could threaten the agreement and expose her to additional consequences. This creates a truthfulness incentive, although it coexists with an incentive to provide useful information.

### 3. Specificity and falsifiability

The reported account identifies Scavino, a White House Christmas party, the approximate date, the Supreme Court-loss context, alcohol consumption, tone, and an exchange in which Ellis objected that remaining in power did not work that way. Specific details make later contradiction or corroboration possible.

### 4. Scavino's unusually close access

Congressional materials place Scavino near Trump throughout the post-election period, involved in communications strategy, social-media drafting, meetings, and January 6 contacts. He was institutionally positioned to know or infer Trump's intentions.

### 5. Temporal consistency with contemporaneous rhetoric and conduct

The reported December 19 conversation occurred during a period when Trump's public account continued to insist he had won, called the result fraudulent, urged supporters not to give up, and promoted January 6 as a decisive event. The statement's timing is congruent with the broader recorded course of conduct.

### 6. Consistency with other insider descriptions

Other White House witnesses described Trump resisting acknowledgment of defeat even after legal avenues closed. This does not independently prove the Scavino conversation, but it reduces the degree to which Ellis's account is behaviorally anomalous.

## Evidence decreasing validity or limiting permissible inference

### 1. Double-hearsay problem as to Trump's state of mind

The clip directly proves what Ellis told prosecutors. It does not directly prove what Scavino said, and even a proven Scavino statement would not automatically prove Trump's actual intent.

### 2. No located contemporaneous corroborating object

No text message, email, party recording, contemporaneous memorandum, calendar annotation, security record paired with a witness account, or third-party recollection has yet been located establishing the exchange.

### 3. Alcohol and elapsed time

Ellis acknowledged that both participants had consumed alcohol. She reported the exchange almost three years later. Neither fact invalidates the account, but both must remain visible as perception and memory variables.

### 4. Cooperation and plea incentives

Ellis had an incentive to be truthful and an incentive to provide prosecutorially useful information. Both incentives operate simultaneously. The ledger must not treat the cooperation posture as either automatic validation or automatic disqualification.

### 5. Ellis's prior reliability impairment

Ellis pleaded guilty to aiding and abetting false statements and publicly acknowledged failing to verify representations she helped advance. That history materially increases the need for external corroboration.

### 6. Public object is an excerpt

The released footage was not the complete proffer session. Missing preceding and following context could qualify the statement, reveal uncertainty, or contain corroborating detail. The excerpt is sufficient to establish that Ellis made the allegation, not sufficient to establish completeness.

### 7. No sworn Scavino account located

Scavino did not provide the January 6 committee with the requested testimony and documents, and no located public response addressed Ellis's account on the merits. This creates an evidence gap, not an adverse inference by itself.

### 8. Eventual transfer occurred

Trump left office on January 20, 2021. This contradicts a literal prediction that he would remain under all circumstances. It does not disprove an earlier intention or attempt, but it limits rhetorical treatment of the statement as a completed outcome.

## Strongest supportable public formulation

> In an authenticated Fulton County proffer recording, Jenna Ellis stated that Dan Scavino told her President Trump would not leave office and that they would remain in power. Scavino's proximity to Trump and the surrounding post-election conduct increase the account's plausibility, but the public record presently lacks Scavino's sworn confirmation, a contemporaneous record of the exchange, or direct proof that he was transmitting a specific Trump instruction rather than his own characterization.

## Promotion boundary

This record may not be used to claim that:

- Ellis personally heard Trump say he would refuse to leave;
- Scavino's alleged statement has been independently proven;
- silence or refusal to testify constitutes an admission;
- the statement itself proves a criminal agreement, coup plan, or use-of-force plan;
- the eventual January 20 departure disproves all earlier intent or attempts;
- contextual consistency substitutes for direct attribution.

Promotion beyond research-candidate status requires the complete proffer, at least one independent corroborating source for the Ellis–Scavino exchange or a sworn Scavino response, claim-level source receipts, contradiction review, and independent review.
