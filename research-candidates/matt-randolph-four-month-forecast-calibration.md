Status: `research_candidate`

## Research question

Across approximately the four months preceding August 23, 2026, how well did Matt Randolph's energy and Iran-conflict forecasts track subsequent observed events when evaluated according to the conditions he actually stated at forecast time?

The evaluation must distinguish:

- unconditional prediction from conditional forecast;
- forecast date from forecast window;
- stated contingencies from post-hoc explanations;
- delay or acceleration caused by a pre-stated contingency;
- world-state outcome from Randolph's later characterization of that outcome;
- independent convergence from common-source repetition;
- expertise/credential context from evidentiary confirmation.

## Evaluation rule

A forecast is not scored as a simple date hit/miss when the original source explicitly stated conditions that would alter timing or outcome.

For each forecast:

1. preserve the original source object and publication timestamp;
2. extract the forecast proposition without strengthening it;
3. extract every stated contingency and its predicted effect;
4. independently reconstruct relevant world-state transitions;
5. determine whether each contingency occurred and when;
6. classify the forecast state using the transition-calculus forecast adapter;
7. preserve unresolved provenance and convergence questions explicitly;
8. score mechanism fit separately from endpoint accuracy.
