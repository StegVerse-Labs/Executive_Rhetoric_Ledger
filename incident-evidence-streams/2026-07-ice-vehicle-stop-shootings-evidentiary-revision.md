# Evidentiary Revision: July 2026 ICE Vehicle-Stop Shootings and Reported Suspension

## Scope

This revision covers three linked records:

1. fatal shooting of Lorenzo Salgado Araujo in the Houston area;
2. fatal shooting of Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero in Biddeford, Maine;
3. reported ICE suspension or limitation of immigration-related vehicle stops after the two shootings.

This document supersedes any earlier summary that treated the incidents as a single settled narrative. It preserves government claims, witness and family accounts, independently observable facts, and unresolved gaps as separate evidence branches.

## Reconstructable five dimensions

### Who

- Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, age 52, identified in reporting as the person killed in the Houston-area incident.
- Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero, age 26, identified in reporting as the person killed in Biddeford.
- ICE officers involved in each operation; identities were not established in the reviewed public reporting.
- DHS and ICE as the agencies issuing public accounts and controlling relevant internal records.
- Witnesses, family members, local officials, state investigators, journalists, and protest participants as external evidence sources.

### What

- Two people were fatally shot by ICE personnel during vehicle-stop or vehicle-interdiction operations six days apart.
- Reporting states neither person was the intended target of the underlying operation.
- DHS described each shooting as connected to a perceived vehicle threat or public-safety danger.
- Witnesses or family representatives disputed material portions of the federal accounts.
- Reuters reported on July 14, 2026 that ICE suspended immigration-related vehicle stops after the shootings.

### Why

The operative reasons remain separated by evidence posture:

- **Federal stated reason:** officers fired because they perceived a threat involving the vehicle or public safety.
- **Reported institutional reason for the later directive:** tactical reassessment after two fatal shootings in a short period.
- **Unresolved causal question:** whether the shootings resulted from lawful imminent-threat decisions, avoidable escalation, mistaken targeting, deficient tactics, quota or operational pressure, or another cause.

No final causal or legal finding is made.

### When

- Houston-area shooting: reported July 8, 2026, with reporting describing it as occurring days before the Maine shooting.
- Biddeford shooting: July 13, 2026.
- Reported ICE vehicle-stop suspension: July 14, 2026.

Exact event timestamps, dispatch times, shot times, medical-response times, internal-notification times, and directive-effective time remain missing.

### Where

- Houston area, Texas: precise scene coordinates and complete scene diagram not yet obtained.
- Biddeford, Maine: coastal Maine city approximately 15 miles south of Portland; precise scene coordinates and complete scene diagram not yet obtained.
- Directive scope: reported as nationwide by some outlets, but the primary directive has not been obtained.

## Findings by evidence strength

### High-confidence externally observable findings

- Two fatal ICE shootings occurred within six days during vehicle-related immigration operations.
- The men killed were not reported to be the original targets of the relevant operations.
- No complete public body-camera record was available for the Biddeford event in reporting reviewed.
- Public controversy, protests, and calls for independent investigation followed.
- Reuters reported an ICE suspension of immigration-related vehicle stops on July 14, 2026.

### Government-asserted findings requiring primary-record confirmation

- Houston: ICE asserted that Salgado Araujo rammed or attempted to use a vehicle against officers.
- Biddeford: DHS asserted the officer fired while fearing for public safety as Durán Guerrero attempted to flee.
- Directive: ICE described procedures as continually evaluated but declined to disclose tactics.

These statements establish the government's position, not the truth of the underlying claims.

### Material contradictions or unresolved disputes

- Witness or family accounts dispute the characterization of the victims as threatening or aggressive.
- Reporting did not identify public video proving the claimed imminent vehicle threats.
- The reported victims' non-target status raises unresolved questions about identification, pursuit, escalation, and mission scope.
- The reported tactical suspension may indicate institutional recognition of elevated risk, but does not itself establish that either shooting was unlawful.

## Primary and physical evidence still required

- complete body-camera, dash-camera, surveillance, traffic-camera, and bystander-video inventory;
- original unedited media files with metadata and custody receipts;
- officer identities, assignments, training records, and use-of-force reports;
- dispatch, radio, text, email, and command-channel records;
- vehicle telemetry, damage documentation, trajectory analysis, shell-casing and ballistic records;
- scene photographs, diagrams, autopsy and medical records;
- warrants, target packets, surveillance plans, and mission authorizations;
- complete witness interviews and prior inconsistent statements;
- the ICE vehicle-stop suspension directive, issuer, distribution list, effective time, exceptions, and supersession history.

## Governing conclusion

The strongest present finding is not that either federal or witness account is conclusively true. It is that two lethal operations and the resulting tactical policy change cannot yet be fully reconstructed from publicly available agency evidence. The evidence stream must therefore preserve the unresolved gaps and identify DHS and ICE as custodians of the primary records needed to resolve them.
