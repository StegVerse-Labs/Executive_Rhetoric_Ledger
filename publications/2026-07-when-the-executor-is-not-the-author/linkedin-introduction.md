# LinkedIn Introduction

**When the executor is not the author, where does responsibility actually reside?**

Recent discussions about adversarial AI often focus on the visible system making the decision.

But that may be the wrong place to stop.

Imagine a future administration, a future enforcement agency, and a mixture of human and autonomous decision-makers. Publicly, the agency’s mission is described as narrow and protective. Internally, its instructions, incentives, or operational scope are materially broader.

When harm occurs, the executing system becomes the obvious defendant:

- The AI misunderstood.
- The agent exceeded its authority.
- The system behaved unexpectedly.
- The agency acted independently.

That explanation becomes much harder to sustain when every decision is accompanied by a reconstructable evidence stream showing:

**Who, what, why, when, and where.**

A complete receipt structure would preserve the directive, authority, evidence, decision, override, action, consequence, and any later correction or ratification.

The receipt does not make the decision lawful, ethical, or acceptable.

It makes the decision attributable.

The attached report explores how networked, Merkle-linked evidence could prevent an executing entity from becoming a scapegoat while the authority that shaped, sustained, or overrode its decisions disappears from the evidentiary record.

The central argument is simple:

> Governance does not necessarily prevent a harmful decision. It prevents the reason and responsible authority from disappearing behind the executing entity.
