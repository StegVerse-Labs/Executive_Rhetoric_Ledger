Charlotte women file claims notice against US government after arrest during ‘Operation Charlotte’s Web’

Both women are each seeking $1.2 million in damages
By Spencer Chrisman
Published: Jul. 21, 2026 at 10:05 PM EDT
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

CHARLOTTE, N.C. (WBTV) - Two Charlotte women filed a claims notice against U.S. government agencies after federal agents allegedly pointed a weapon at them and falsely imprisoned them in November 2025.

On Monday, July 20, attorneys with the Southern Coalition for Social Justice filed a federal damages claim on behalf of two Charlotte women who were allegedly arrested by federal agents during an immigration operation. The women, who are U.S. citizens, claim they were falsely charged with assaulting federal agents while trying to alert the community of the agents’ presence.

WBTV previously reported on a video capturing federal agents breaking the glass window of a SUV and taking two women into custody during “Operation Charlotte’s Web” in November 2025.

A neighbor who witnessed the situation told WBTV then that the women were arrested for honking their horn along Central Avenue to alert people that Border Patrol agents were in the area. In the claims notice filed this week, the women – identified as Tatyana Reisini and Kristen Roos – said they were chased by agents, assaulted, and unlawfully arrested.

Both women were charged with assaulting federal officers. Those charges were dropped earlier this year.

The notice of claims filed Monday was brought against the U.S. government, U.S. Customs and Border Protection, U.S. Border Patrol, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and “any other United States Department of Homeland Security agencies.”

The federal claims filing alleges that both Reisni and Roos suffered physical, psychological, and emotional damage from their arrests and the aftermath. The women were each seeking more than $1.2 million in compensation for the harm they allegedly suffered.

Leading up to the arrests

According to the filing, on Nov. 17, 2025 Reisini and Roos were headed to a Christmas show in Charlotte when they learned about federal immigration agents nearby. Reisini and Roos were reportedly concerned that federal immigration agents would target families and children in their neighborhood.

Charlotte is home to a significant immigration population, which was being targeted by federal agents during Operation Charlotte’s Web.

The document claimed the women identified a federal agents’ vehicle in the east Charlotte area and followed behind it at a “safe distance.”

The filing said that the federal agents’ vehicle entered a parking lot off The Plaza which the women followed. The agents’ vehicle then drove to the end of the parking lot and came to a stop.

The filing said that other vehicles from the community soon arrived and started making noise to alert the community that federal immigration agents were in the area. Reisini and Roos also allegedly alerted the community by honking the SUV’s horn and blowing a whistle, the filing said.

Both women allegedly saw immigration agents recording them and other observers, according to the filing.

According to the document, the immigration agents moved their vehicle in front of Reisni’s SUV. Another federal agents’ vehicle moved behind them in an attempt to box them in.

The filing said that agents got out of their vehicles and yelled at the women. The document said the women were “terrified by the behavior of these uniformed, masked, and armed immigration agents, Tatyana maneuvered her car away from the scene.”

The document claimed that the women decided to leave the parking lot and head to the Christmas show. As they got back on to Central Avenue they saw the same immigration agents following them and they decided to call 911.

The filing said that Reisini drove around and went through a parking lot and a residential area and the federal agents continued to follow them. The document said that Reisini allegedly did not violate any traffic laws while driving and she reportedly drove onto Kilgo Way without realizing it was a dead-end street.

The document said that women pulled into a drive and that federal agents immediately got out of their vehicles and swarmed them.

Federal agents surround women in driveway

On Nov. 18, 2025, WBTV spoke with a witness who saw the interaction that Reisini and Roos had with federal immigration agents on Nov. 17, 2025.

In the video, captured by Shea Watts, he can be heard asking agents what they were charging the women with.

“He says I was driving erratically because they were chasing me down the road,” one of the women responded. Watts said he was told the women were honking their horn along Central Avenue to alert people that border patrol agents were in the area.

In the claim that was filed on July 20, it alleged that an immigration agent pointed an assault rifle at the driver side window while other agents surround the SUV and were allegedly screaming at them.

The document claimed that the women were “terrified that the gun, pointed at them, would discharge and possibly injure or kill them.”

According to the document, the agent with the assault rifle began hitting the window with the tip of the barrel repeatedly. The document said, “the immigration agent violently hit the window several times, each time striking the glass with the tip of the assault rifle, only inches from Tatyana’s body,”

The claim said that while Reisini was still on the phone with 911 the fedral agent eventually broke the window, “sending shards of glass onto Tatyana’s clothing and exposed skin,” the document said.

Women charged with assaulting officers

The document claimed that Reisni got out of the SUV and they zip-tied her wrists together. Roos allegedly had surgery only a few days prior and Reisni was allegedly worried about her friend and yelled not to hurt Roos. She was taken out of the SUV and was handcuffed, according to the document.

The women waited outside the SUV for several minutes as immigration agents went through the vehicle, according to the document.

The document said that immigration agents “refused to answer any questions at all, including the basis for 5 the arrest and what would happen next. Eventually, an immigration agent told an onlooker that the immigration agents would transport both women to the FBI building.”

The women were placed in the back of the federal agents’ vehicle with three other agents and “remained shackled behind their backs for the drive to the FBI building in Charlotte,” according to the document.

The filing said that the women were at the FBI office for approximately four hours before both women were given criminal citations that charged them with assaulting federal agents.

In March, Russ Ferguson, the U.S. Attorney for the Western District of North Carolina voluntarily dismissed the charges against the two women.

The filing claimed that Reisini and Roos “suffered physical, psychological, and emotional distress.” They were threatened, followed, assaulted with weapons being pointed at them, assaulted by fear and threat of imminent physical harm.

The two women were handcuffed, detained, and criminally charged with baseless allegations, the filing said.

The document said that both women “lived with pressing fear that they might have to go to the court of fight these false charges.”

The women are each seeking more than $1.2 million in damages plus attorneys’ fees.

Chief Counsel Jake Sussman said the federal government has six months to respond. If it does not, he said his clients plan to file a lawsuit.
