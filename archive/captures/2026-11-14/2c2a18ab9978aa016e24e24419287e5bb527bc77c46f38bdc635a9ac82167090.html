U.S. Border Patrol hosts tracking class

Release Date
Mon, 11/09/2026

GRAND FORKS, N.D. - U.S. Border Patrol, Grand Forks Sector, hosted a multiagency tracking class from October 20–22. The three-day course brought together regional partners to sharpen operational capabilities across the unique terrain of GFN’s area of responsibility. The specialized training is vital along the northern border, where agents and local law enforcement partners must be prepared to track during life-threatening conditions brought on by the region's harsh winter climate. Participants honed their tracking skills and became better prepared to conduct search operations for lost individuals. These skillsets go beyond apprehending border crossers and underscore the critical, life-saving commitment to community safety as winter approaches. 

Participating agencies included Pembina County Sheriff’s Office, Grand Forks County Sheriff’s Office, Grand Forks Police Department, Kittson County Sheriff’s Office, Canadian Law Enforcement Partners and the North Dakota Highway Patrol. Given the remote nature of our northern border operations, these joint partnerships are vital.  USBP frequently relies on our partner agencies for various law enforcement situations; maintaining these consistent, collaborative, working relationships is essential to our mission.

The course blended three days of intensive classroom instruction, practical exercises, and scenario-based training.  Participants covered the fundamentals of tracking people and included: establishing sign descriptions, high sign and low sign, reading footprints and grass trails, and understanding the various components required to track individuals across diverse terrain. "Training is a critical element of mission readiness. Many skills, such as tracking/sign-cutting are perishable.  Opportunities like this provide agents and officers the ability to learn new principles and build upon practical experience,” said Acting Chief Patrol Agent, Michael W. Hanson. “Beyond apprehending illegal aliens and criminals, these vital skills increase law enforcement’s ability to locate missing hunters, lost hikers, endangered children, etc.  With winter right around the corner, this is more significant than ever.”

On the final day, the training culminated with dynamic field scenarios at the Pembina Gorge.  Teams were tested on their ability to track and bring three distinct scenarios to a logical law enforcement conclusion: wanted felon scenario, lost hiker group scenario, and an illegal cross-border group scenario.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) is America's frontline: the nation's largest law enforcement organization and the world's first unified border management agency. The 70,000+ men and women of CBP protect America on the ground, in the air and on the seas. We enforce safe, lawful travel and trade and ensure our country's economic prosperity. We enhance the nation's security through innovation, intelligence, collaboration and trust.
