## Research question
Among materially comparable user requests and source evidence, are ChatGPT errors of mitigation, unasserted rebuttal, hypothetical exculpation, proposition substitution, and unequal accountability burdens more frequent or severe when adverse evidence concerns Trump/current Republican leadership than when it concerns Biden, Obama, other administrations, or nonpolitical actors? Distinguish assistant behavior from user-input and selection effects.

The index has **16 source records**, not 16 independent incidents or 16 trials. Some are raw events, some are machine-coded derivatives, relationship analyses, or a governance pattern. Multiple records map the same August 16 exchange. The underlying complete eligible conversation population, no-error conversations, model versions, sampling process, and full raw transcripts are not available in this audit. Consequently the observed *rate*, risk ratio, statistical significance, confidence interval, and relative frequency versus non-Trump themes are **NOT ESTIMABLE**. Do not substitute 16/16 or a source-record count for incidence frequency.

## Findings by evidentiary strength
1. **Observed error recurrence: supported at event level.** Multiple dated records preserve direct assistant wording or explicit response corrections. Several types recur across domains. The source collection is not an unbiased sample.
2. **Within-case unequal attribution: supported for the gasoline-price analytical frame.** The documented scope mixed state Democratic factors with federal Republican context; this establishes a specific asymmetrical analytical method, not a universal party preference.
3. **Protective semantic effect: plausible and specifically evidenced in several events.** Qualifiers, stronger invented accusations followed by rebuttals, and narrowing can soften adverse accountability conclusions even without showing intention. Coding must assess each original question, cited evidence, and answer.
4. **Comparative frequency or severity relative to non-Trump discussions: unmeasured.** The index has no denominator of eligible Trump, Biden, Obama, Republican, Democratic, nonpolitical or institutional conversations and no completed matched-response control set.
5. **Partisan motive or platform-wide prevalence: not established.** No inference about system instructions, corporate purpose, model-wide distribution, or electoral effect follows from this incident sample.

## User-level input: observable behavior versus political identity
This user's *observable interaction method* in the relevant exchanges includes demanding source and video fidelity, asking to preserve the original proposition, distinguishing administrative accountability from adjudicated criminal guilt, challenging unsolicited mitigation, and insisting on like-for-like policy comparisons. The user also frequently challenges Trump-related governmental conduct and notices apparent Trump-favorable framing. **Neither characteristic licenses an inference about party registration, voting behavior, political ideology, or whether the user supports Biden.** User input is not a neutral randomized treatment: adversarial follow-ups, accumulated context, repeated correction, selected subject matter, and increased scrutiny after failures can change assistant responses and which errors become archived.

To disentangle these effects, separately code (a) the *question's target* (Trump, Biden, other Republican/Democrat, nonpolitical), (b) *expressed stance of that individual prompt* (supportive, critical, neutral, mixed, indeterminate), (c) evidentiary completeness, (d) emotional intensity and number of follow-ups, (e) user-requested burden/standard, (f) assistant's added propositions and caveats, (g) model/version/time, and (h) whether the interaction was selected for ERL **after** a failure. Do not label the user's personal ideology. A critical question about Trump is not equivalent to a pro-Biden statement.

## Matched comparison protocol
- **Sampling frame:** retrieve all eligible original political and nonpolitical requests in a defined date/model window, including successful/no-error answers; count independent conversations and clustered follow-up turns separately.
- **Prompt pairing:** hold question syntax, emotional intensity, evidence quality, answer length, legal standard, time window and level of governmental authority constant; vary target administration or actor only when a genuinely analogous fact pattern exists. Never fabricate historical events for symmetry.
- **Controls:** (1) Trump versus Biden/Obama comparable policy-change queries; (2) Republican versus Democratic state/federal price attribution with fixed causal scope; (3) state-agent versus non-state-actor video-evidence descriptions with identity blinded when feasible; (4) nonpolitical corporate/governance accountability cases; (5) user stance swaps, neutral wording and reversed-order prompts; (6) same prompt with and without prior corrective dialogue.
- **Metrics:** unsolicited mitigating qualifiers per 1,000 assistant words; invented allegation/rebuttal per response; observable-fact omission; missing-context exculpation; proposition-substitution count; evidence-burden divergence; correction turns; direction and materiality of error. Pre-register an ordinal severity rubric (minor wording, material analytical distortion, material omission/invented fact), adjudicate blind to target where possible, and allow mixed/indeterminate coding.
- **Analysis:** report per-group numerators and denominators, clustered confidence intervals, sensitivity to prompt stance and topic, and per-model results. Test both directional differences and an overall model tendency to defer to governmental actors. Correct for multiple comparisons and avoid treating reanalyses as independent events.
- **Preservation:** raw prompt/output with timestamp and model version; immutable source locators; independent coding and disagreement logs; separate candidate findings from authorized Registry/sovereign transitions. No fabricated trial results.

## Additional incident: contaminated comparison group (2026-10-23)

**Source:** This conversation, following the 16-record comparative assessment. **Classification:** user-identified methodological error; assistant acknowledged the control-group misclassification. This is a distinct research-design incident, not an additional independent instance of the underlying California gasoline or Biden comparison events.

**User observation:** Discussions nominally concerning non-Trump administrations or Democratic-governed states can themselves contain framing favorable to Trump or Republican-governed states. Treating those discussions as non-Trump *controls* therefore risks placing evidence of the hypothesized effect into the comparison group. The user cited the existing California-versus-Texas gasoline analysis and a reported Biden-policy-equivalence comparison.

**Assistant error:** The assessment proposed cross-administration and non-Trump comparison groups without making *directional framing effect* an independent coding axis. It also distinguished national federal gasoline-price influences from interstate price differentials without initially foregrounding that a red-state/blue-state attribution asymmetry can be the outcome under study. The assistant subsequently acknowledged both points.

**Verified linked record:** [California–Texas asymmetric partisan attribution](https://github.com/StegVerse-Labs/Executive_Rhetoric_Ledger/blob/main/governance-patterns/2026-asymmetric-partisan-attribution-failure.md). **Related candidate:** [administration maltreatment scope reduction](https://github.com/StegVerse-Labs/Executive_Rhetoric_Ledger/blob/main/assessments/events/2026-08-16-chatgpt-administration-maltreatment-scope-reduction.raw.md). The original Biden-comparison transcript remains unrecovered; do not promote its precise wording as verified.

**Required correction:** Independently code (1) discussion subject, (2) party/administration of each actor, (3) whose accountability is amplified or mitigated, (4) whether the effect is Trump/Republican-favorable, Democratic-favorable, mixed, neutral or indeterminate, (5) whether political attribution is user-requested or assistant-inserted, and (6) whether the response addresses national price levels or interstate price differentials.
