# Governed Review — PIT-MODERN-2026-IRAN-JORDAN-FIRSTNET

## Review status

`evidence-acquisition-required`

This review accepts the July 28–30, 2026 Iran–Jordan–FirstNet record for catalogue, source preservation, timestamp normalization, contradiction testing, and governed primary-record acquisition only.

It does **not** establish:

- that Iran compromised AT&T, FirstNet, Astound, or another U.S. communications network;
- that the reported network degradation was staged, malicious, coordinated, national, or causally related to the missile launch;
- that the July 22 federal cyber advisory was bait;
- that target intelligence was planted or manipulated;
- that the United States induced or orchestrated the Iranian launch;
- that Washington ordered Jordan to delay or issue its statement;
- that Jordan intentionally joined the U.S. war effort;
- that the U.S. response was unlawful, disproportionate, or based only on the publicly disclosed missile event;
- that the missile event was fabricated, permitted, or knowingly tolerated.

## Accepted record posture

The current record supports preservation of the following claim classes as distinct objects:

1. **Physical-event claims** — missiles were launched, defensive systems engaged them, Jordan claimed five interceptions, and no successful effect was publicly reported.
2. **Iranian official claims** — the IRGC claimed responsibility for an operation using an unspecified number of ballistic missiles against two described U.S. target categories in Jordan.
3. **U.S. official characterization** — CENTCOM rapidly described the event as an attempted surprise attack against U.S. forces.
4. **Jordanian official characterization** — Jordan later described its defensive interception through its own official carrier.
5. **Executive rhetoric** — the president used punitive and escalatory language before the subsequent strike operation.
6. **Operational inference** — the breadth and sophistication of the U.S. target set support prior contingency planning; they do not alone establish when execution was authorized or what classified inputs shaped the decision.
7. **Firsthand network observation** — an actual FirstNet line and an Astound fixed-broadband connection were reportedly degraded during the antecedent period and later recovered without local equipment changes.
8. **Hypothesis layer** — ordinary contingency activation, accumulated-conflict response, undisclosed threat inputs, event exploitation, shaped intelligence, and unrelated or staged degradation remain competing explanations.

These claim classes may be linked by time and adjacency. They may not be collapsed into causation without claim-level receipts.

## Promotion tests

### Test A — network incident

A network-side incident may be promoted only when provider, regulatory, device, or independent telemetry establishes service degradation beyond a consumer-device explanation.

### Test B — common cause

A common cause linking FirstNet and Astound may be promoted only when route, facility, power, backhaul, maintenance, or cyber evidence identifies a shared dependency or coordinated event.

### Test C — Iranian attribution

Iranian attribution requires incident-specific indicators, infrastructure access evidence, authenticated government assessment, or independently reproducible forensic linkage. General threat advisories are insufficient.

### Test D — Jordanian coordination

Narrative priority may be described from publication order. U.S. control, requested delay, persuasion, coercion, or deliberate Jordanian affiliation requires communications or approval-chain evidence.

### Test E — orchestration

Orchestration requires evidence that an actor helped create, shape, permit, time, or induce the launch event. A prepared response, rapid rhetoric, and event exploitation are not alone sufficient.

### Test F — response basis

The missile event may be described as a public activation event for a pre-existing response architecture. Claims that classified cyber, personal-threat, maritime, intelligence, or other inputs materially caused the selected campaign require authorization-stage records or attributable official disclosure.

## Contradiction rules

- `five missiles intercepted` must not be rewritten as `five missiles launched`.
- `several missiles` must remain an unspecified quantity.
- `targeted` must not be rewritten as `hit` or `destroyed`.
- `attempted surprise attack` must remain an official characterization unless independently reconstructed.
- `prepared strike architecture` must not be rewritten as `preauthorized execution`.
- `FirstNet line degraded` must not be rewritten as `FirstNet system failed`.
- `contemporaneous Iranian cyber warning` must not be rewritten as `Iran caused the outage`.
- `Jordan spoke later` must not be rewritten as `Jordan was forced to speak`.
- `event exploitation` must not be rewritten as `event orchestration`.

## Authority boundary

Automation may discover, archive, hash, translate, timestamp-normalize, deduplicate, cluster, create adjacency records, identify contradictions, and propose source objects.

Automation may not promote attribution, orchestration, illegality, proportionality, alliance intent, or common-cause findings. Those require governed human review, claim-level evidence, explicit contradiction treatment, and a passed validation receipt.

## Current disposition

The candidate remains valuable because the public narrative contains a measurable mismatch among a small defeated launch, rapid intent framing, delayed allied disclosure, a broad pre-existing response architecture, escalated executive rhetoric, and unexplained public-safety communications degradation.

That mismatch justifies structured acquisition. It does not resolve the missing causal links.
