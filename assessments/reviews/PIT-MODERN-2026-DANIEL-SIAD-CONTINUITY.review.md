## Review determination

The record is accepted for catalogue inclusion, source acquisition, evidence-custody reconstruction, and cross-jurisdictional continuity analysis.

The record is not accepted as proof of homicide, suicide, coercion, concealment, trafficking liability, conspiracy, obstruction, or intentional destruction of evidence.

The governing question is limited to recoverability:

> What evidence, testimony, records, devices, accounts, business materials, and cross-border requests existed before the death, what was preserved afterward, and what investigative capacity—if any—was lost?

## Accepted propositions

The installed secondary receipts may support only that:

- Reuters reported Daniel Siad's death outside Paris;
- Reuters reported that French prosecutors opened a cause-of-death investigation and ordered an autopsy;
- Reuters reported that his name appeared repeatedly in released DOJ Epstein-related files;
- Reuters and other reporting described allegations against him, his denials, and his lawyer's procedural statement;
- the death creates a legitimate records-preservation and investigation-continuity question.

These propositions remain subject to replacement or refinement by French primary records and document-level DOJ mapping.

## Blocked propositions

The following conclusions are prohibited unless separately supported by admissible primary evidence and governed review:

1. that Siad was murdered;
2. that Siad died by suicide;
3. that the death was caused by, connected to, or intended to affect Epstein-related scrutiny;
4. that any government, private actor, victim, witness, journalist, or network participant caused or concealed the death;
5. that document-reference frequency proves criminal involvement;
6. that evidence was destroyed, removed, altered, or intentionally left unsecured;
7. that any allegation of rape, trafficking, recruitment, or conspiracy was proven;
8. that Jean-Luc Brunel's death and Siad's death establish a coordinated pattern;
9. that public suspicion substitutes for evidence.
