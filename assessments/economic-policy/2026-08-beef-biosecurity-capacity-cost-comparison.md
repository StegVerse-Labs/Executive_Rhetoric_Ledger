# Beef/Biosecurity Policy — Preventive Capacity vs Emergency Response Cost Comparison

Status: `research_candidate`
As-of: 2026-08-22
Causal finding authorized: bounded only
Corruption/favoritism finding authorized: false

## Purpose
Test whether policy reduced standing animal-health prevention/detection capacity and later purchased overlapping emergency capabilities at materially higher cost. This is a function-level comparison, not a claim that the reductions caused New World screwworm or any other outbreak.

## Quantitative observations

### FAO preventive-capacity scale
FAO's Director-General stated:
- the United States provided around 90% of ECTAD funding over roughly 20 years;
- U.S.-supported functions included early warning, field surveillance, laboratory networks, risk communication, and emergency response;
- funding losses were around $60M per year;
- technical prevention/control/rapid-response capacity was reduced to a level he characterized as almost terminated.

The same statement referenced a U.S. support increase to $468M over five years approximately three years earlier. That implies a nominal average commitment near $93.6M/year for that five-year package, although the relationship between that package and the cited $60M/year loss requires award-level reconstruction before treating the two figures as directly comparable.

### APHIS personnel scale
Reuters reported two snapshots:
- May 2025: 1,377 APHIS departures (~16% at that point), including about 400 Veterinary Services personnel; 13/23 area veterinarian posts implicated in departure plans.
- OIG-derived June 2025 period: 2,105 APHIS departures, about 25% of the agency.

The increase between snapshots indicates the workforce contraction continued after the first outbreak-readiness warnings were public.

### Emergency spending presently identified
Known or publicly announced NWS-specific streams in the current investigation include at least:
- ~$105M — NWS Grand Challenge, 40 projects;
- up to ~$44.096M — Dynamic Aviation NWS aviation-support contract;
- ~$610M — Moore Air Base sterile-fly production facility total project cost;
- additional international sterile-fly conversion/dispersal and response funding not yet normalized into this table.

These figures exceed $759M before all other identified NWS expenditures are normalized. This total must not be interpreted as 'replacement cost of the cuts': the Moore Air Base facility addresses a pre-existing infrastructure gap, and the Grand Challenge includes novel R&D rather than only substituted standing capacity.

## Material new procurement detail

- solicitation: `12639526R0005`;
- awardee: Dynamic Aviation Group;
- awarding organization: USDA APHIS / Marketing and Regulatory Programs Business Services;
- funding office: APHIS Plant Protection and Quarantine;
- pricing: fixed price;
- extent competed: not competed;
- solicitation procedure: sole source;
- number of bids: 1;
- cited other-than-full-and-open basis: urgency / delay could result in injury;
- subcontracting plan: not required;
- business-size determination: large business;
- potential value: ~$44.096M;
- observed obligation: ~$17.1M.

This materially narrows the next procurement question: obtain the actual justification-and-approval document, market-research record, alternatives considered, and contract modifications. The metadata alone does not establish that Dynamic Aviation was uniquely capable or that the sole-source decision was improper.

## Grand Challenge allocation constraint
The FY2026 NOFO stated:
- up to $100M available;
- about 20 awards initially estimated;
- awards generally not anticipated to exceed $5M;
- APHIS reserved authority to exceed $5M for projects with major impact or multiple priority topics.

The final June 2026 announcement selected 40 projects totaling about $105M from 226 applications requesting about $664M. The published award-selection list identifies recipients/projects but does not provide an amount for each project. Therefore exact private-recipient benefit remains unresolved even though the total funding envelope and general award-size expectation are known.

## Current inference boundary
Supported inference:
- preventive and response capacity was materially reduced in both an international surveillance layer and APHIS staffing;
- later NWS emergency spending expanded some overlapping capabilities;
- this creates a valid cost-effectiveness and resilience question.

Not yet supported:
- headline emergency spending minus $60M/year equals the cost of reversing the cuts;
- cuts caused NWS spread;
- emergency procurement beneficiaries received improper preference;
- Moore Air Base spending is a replacement for a capability eliminated by this administration.
