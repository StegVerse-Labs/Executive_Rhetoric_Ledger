# OWS Slaoui/Mango 42-to-60-Day Discriminator Note

Status: `research_candidate`
Date: 2026-08-10
Canonical case: `ERL-SCA-2026-07-29-FAUCI-HSGAC`
Canonical coordination: Issue #47 / PR #48
Supporting lane: OWS/FDA 60-day safety-window reconstruction

## Purpose

Test whether opposition to FDA's 60-day COVID-19 vaccine safety-follow-up requirement was a uniform Operation Warp Speed scientific position, a political/HHS position, or a mixed internal position. This note also tests Paul Mango's later characterization that FDA `extended` an earlier 42-day standard to 60 days.

No motive, culpability, concealment, or injury-causation finding is authorized.

## Finding 1 — Slaoui publicly described 42 days to two months as the expected adverse-event window before the October guidance fight

Source: CNN transcript, 2020-09-11, interview clip with Moncef Slaoui.
Locator: https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/sitroom/date/2020-09-11/segment/02

Observed:
- Asked when a vaccine could be considered safe enough, Slaoui said that based on FDA databases the overwhelming majority of vaccine-associated adverse events occur within about 42 days after completing the immunization regimen, `and maybe two months after completing`.
- He expressly acknowledged that later events could still occur, but described them as extremely rare.

Evaluation effect:
- `WEAKEN` proposition that OWS's chief scientific adviser regarded 60 days as obviously unnecessary or scientifically baseless before FDA formalized the criterion.
- `STRENGTHEN` proposition that the 42-day-to-two-month interval was already part of Slaoui's public safety reasoning before October 6.

## Finding 2 — Slaoui later expressly endorsed the 60-day requirement and rejected evidence of deliberate Pfizer delay

Source: ABC `This Week` transcript, 2020-11-22.
Locator: https://abcnews.com/Politics/week-transcript-11-22-20-president-elect-biden/story?id=74345705

Observed:
- Asked whether Pfizer had deliberately delayed reporting its successful trial until after the election, Slaoui said he did not know Pfizer's internal specifics because OWS had an arm's-length relationship with Pfizer.
- Slaoui then said that asking for 60 days of follow-up after completion of immunization to understand short-term and predictable long-term safety was `an appropriate decision`.
- He said that, as far as he knew, no specific action had been taken to delay the vaccine.

Evaluation effect:
- `WEAKEN MATERIALLY` proposition that OWS scientific leadership as a whole considered the 60-day requirement an improper political delay.
- `STRENGTHEN` hypothesis of internal divergence: Mango/HHS/White House political officials could oppose the 60-day criterion while the OWS chief scientific adviser accepted it as scientifically appropriate.
- `DISAMBIGUATE` Trump/Pfizer post-election rhetoric from Slaoui's scientific assessment.

## Finding 3 — Mango's 2022 account describes opposition but attributes the scientific percentages to Slaoui second-hand

Source: BioSpace interview with Paul Mango, 2022-03-14.
Locator: https://www.biospace.com/behind-the-scenes-at-operation-warp-speed-with-paul-mango

Observed:
- Mango said OWS members wondered whether FDA's decision was politically motivated when follow-up was changed from 42 to 60 days.
- Mango said he consulted Slaoui, who allegedly told him approximately 95% of adverse events would emerge by 42 days and 99% by 60 days.
- Mango said Peter Marks later told him the longer period was intended to improve vaccine confidence; Mango considered the intention sound but called the safety measure `arcane`.

Evidence-status distinction:
- Mango's own opposition / political-suspicion statement: first-person Mango account.
- `95% by 42 / 99% by 60` percentages: **Mango's attribution to Slaoui**, not independently verified as a Slaoui statement in the sources located so far.
- Direct Slaoui sources located so far use qualitative language (`overwhelming majority`) and endorse 60 days as appropriate; they do not reproduce the 95/99 percentages.

Evaluation effect:
- `STRENGTHEN` evidence that Mango personally opposed/discounted the incremental delay.
- `WEAKEN` any treatment of the 95/99 percentages as independently verified Slaoui evidence until a primary Slaoui source or cited dataset is found.

## Finding 4 — Mango's `42 days -> 60 days` description should not yet be encoded as a formal FDA requirement change

Sources:
- FDA, June 30, 2020 `Development and Licensure of Vaccines to Prevent COVID-19` guidance announcement.
- FDA, October 2020 EUA guidance and current archived explanatory material.
- HRSA National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program resources and Vaccine Injury Table descriptions.

Observed:
- FDA's June licensure guidance described required manufacturing, nonclinical, clinical, and post-licensure safety evidence, but the searchable official material located in this pass does not establish a universal 42-day EUA safety-follow-up requirement that was later formally changed to 60 days.
- FDA's October EUA framework expressly required a median of at least two months of Phase 3 follow-up after completion of the full vaccination regimen.
- HRSA explains that Vaccine Injury Table time windows identify periods in which listed injuries receive a statutory presumption of causation for compensation. Those windows are injury- and vaccine-specific legal presumptions; they are not themselves a universal clinical-trial safety standard.
- COVID-19 vaccine injuries are handled under the Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program, not the ordinary VICP table.

Evaluation effect:
- `DISAMBIGUATE / CORRECT`: do not treat `42 days` as an established prior FDA EUA requirement merely because Mango references the Vaccine Injury Compensation framework or conventional adverse-event windows.
- Required next proof: locate the exact pre-October FDA guidance, sponsor communication, or protocol language Mango was referring to and compare it against the October 6 EUA guidance.

## Finding 5 — OWS was intentionally separated from FDA authorization authority

Sources:
- BioSpace Mango interview, 2022-03-14.
- FDA public materials.

Observed:
- Mango said FDA was the one agency deliberately not represented inside OWS because FDA would ultimately grant or deny EUAs.
- FDA materials state that career FDA scientists and physicians made the EUA decision.

Evaluation effect:
- `STRENGTHEN` authority separation between OWS execution and FDA regulatory authorization.
- `STRENGTHEN` significance of any White House/HHS attempts to influence FDA guidance because such influence crossed an intentionally preserved institutional boundary.

## Bounded conclusion

This pass materially changes the internal-OWS picture. Mango's later account establishes that he viewed the 60-day extension skeptically, but direct Slaoui statements show the chief scientific adviser publicly treated the 42-day-to-two-month range as scientifically relevant before the guidance dispute and later called the 60-day requirement appropriate. Therefore ERL should no longer describe `OWS` as a unitary actor opposing 60 days. The evidence currently supports a more discriminating model in which political/HHS officials and OWS scientific leadership may have held materially different positions.

No motive, criminal, concealment, or injury-causation promotion is authorized from these findings alone.
