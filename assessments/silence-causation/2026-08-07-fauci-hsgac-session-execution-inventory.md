## Required discriminators added by this session

1. **Blanket-refusal baseline.** Compute the observed refusal rate for harmless and substantive questions before assigning discriminatory value to an individual invocation.
2. **Question specificity is not proof of committee evidence.** A detailed question may preserve a candidate evidentiary predicate, but the predicate must be captured independently.
3. **Authority assignment control.** For each atomic question, identify the actor with formal or documented de facto authority at the relevant timestamp and flag possible authority misassignment.
4. **Personnel lifecycle control.** Departure, retirement, reassignment, sanction, indictment, later appointment, and continued domain participation are separate event classes and must not be collapsed into a common motive.
5. **Transition clustering control.** A cluster of departures near an administration change is an observation; broad restructuring is a required competing explanation unless specific causal evidence exists.
6. **Records chronology control.** Distinguish deletion language, actual deletion, destruction of a federal record, private-channel use, pending FOIA/preservation duty, and intent to defeat disclosure.
7. **Channel-purpose control.** Receiving information over a private channel does not establish knowledge that the sender selected the channel to evade records law.
8. **Role-characterization conflict.** Compare sworn descriptions of a staff member's function with primary records of actual tasking/briefing activity, without treating title alone as proof.
9. **Gain-of-function semantic normalization.** Keep generic biological gain of function, regulated P3CO/ePPP research, and pandemic causation as distinct propositions.
10. **Independent-answer reconstruction.** The strongest question clusters are those answerable from documents or third parties even if the witness refuses.
