# OWS 42-Day Provenance and 60-Day Scientific-Basis Note

Status: `research_candidate`
Date: 2026-08-10
Canonical case: `ERL-SCA-2026-07-29-FAUCI-HSGAC`
Canonical coordination: Issue #47 / PR #48
Related lanes: direct presidential pressure; OWS/FDA safety-window dispute; Mango/Slaoui discriminator

## Purpose

Resolve the provenance of the repeatedly cited `42 days` benchmark and distinguish it from FDA's October 2020 `median two months / 60 days after completion of the full vaccination regimen` EUA evidentiary requirement.

This note does not make a motive, criminal-liability, concealment, or injury-causation finding.

## Finding 1 — no primary FDA source found establishing 42 days as the pre-October COVID-vaccine EUA requirement

Searches of FDA's June 30, 2020 COVID-vaccine development/licensure guidance surfaces and contemporaneous FDA testimony found efficacy, safety, manufacturing, clinical-development, licensure, post-licensure, and case-by-case EUA criteria, but did not identify a fixed universal `42-day` COVID-vaccine EUA follow-up requirement.

The June 30 guidance was aimed primarily at traditional licensure and said any EUA would be evaluated case by case based on product/population and the totality of the available evidence.

Current classification:
- `42 days as pre-existing binding FDA COVID EUA rule`: **not established**.
- `42 days as historical/scientific adverse-event benchmark`: **supported**, below.

## Finding 2 — Slaoui's direct September 2020 statement treats 42 days as something he was told from FDA databases, not as his own formal rule

**Source:** CNN transcript, 2020-09-11, Dr. Moncef Slaoui interview clip.

Slaoui said that if one looks at FDA databases, the overwhelming majority of vaccine adverse events occur within the first period after immunization. Critically, his wording was that he **"was told 42 days"**, and then added **"maybe two months"** after completing the immunization regimen. He immediately cautioned that this did not mean events could not happen later; very rare events could.

Implications:
- The direct Slaoui source supports a scientific/historical observation window spanning about six weeks to two months.
- It does not support a provenance claim that Slaoui personally derived or promulgated a binding 42-day FDA requirement.
- It does not independently support Mango's later precise `95% by 42 / 99% by 60` attribution.

## Finding 3 — FDA vaccine leadership later articulated the scientific rationale more precisely

**Source:** Peter Marks, American Medical Association COVID-19 vaccine update, 2020-2021 public remarks.

Marks explained that FDA requested a minimum median of two months of follow-up after the final vaccination because **most adverse events with vaccines appear within the first 42 days**. In a separate AMA update he described the estimate as about **95% of serious adverse events becoming apparent within about six weeks and certainly within two months**.

Implications:
- `42 days` is best represented as the approximate outer range in which most known serious vaccine adverse events historically emerge, not as a universal pre-existing EUA legal requirement.
- `60 days` was a deliberate additional evidentiary margin designed to observe events beginning in the six-week window and to increase confidence in the absence/presence of important safety events before mass deployment.

## Finding 4 — contemporaneous FDA scientists Gruber and Krause published a strong rationale for two months

**Source:** Philip R. Krause and Marion F. Gruber, `Emergency Use Authorization of Covid Vaccines — Safety and Efficacy Follow-up Considerations`, New England Journal of Medicine, published 2020-10-16.

They wrote that:
- plausibly vaccine-linked adverse events generally start within about six weeks after vaccination;
- a median two-month follow-up allows immune-mediated adverse events beginning within that six-week period to be observed and evaluated;
- two months increases confidence in absence when no event is seen;
- FDA generally requires substantially longer safety follow-up for full licensure (they cite at least six months for serious/medically attended adverse events);
- given novel vaccine technologies, longer EUA follow-up could be argued for, but two months was a justified emergency compromise;
- shortening the minimum could undermine scientific credibility of the EUA decision.

This is strong evidence that two months was not merely a communications delay or arbitrary confidence theater; FDA vaccine scientists articulated a substantive safety and efficacy rationale.

## Finding 5 — Mango later used two materially different framings of the same 60-day requirement

### Mango 2022 BioSpace framing

Mango said FDA changed follow-up `from 42 days to 60`, OWS wondered whether the timing was political, Slaoui allegedly told him ~95% of adverse events would emerge by 42 days and ~99% by 60, and Peter Marks later told him the extra interval was meant to increase vaccine confidence. Mango called the measure `arcane` while acknowledging Marks's intention was sound.

### Mango 2022 Government Executive / public-defense framing

In a separate 2022 interview, Mango characterized the 60-day requirement as evidence that FDA had actually made COVID vaccine review **more stringent** than ordinary vaccine evaluation: more Phase 3 participants and a longer adverse-event observation interval. He used that to argue the vaccines were among the safest ever evaluated by FDA.

### Mango 2022 American Spectator/book-excerpt framing

Mango likewise wrote that the interval from median second dose to EUA had been **extended beyond previous trials**, and used that fact to reject claims that Warp Speed compromised safety standards.

Implication:
- Mango's public record simultaneously supports `the 60-day rule was a real additional safety stringency` and `Mango personally thought the increment was unnecessarily arcane/politically suspicious at the time`.
- ERL should preserve both statements rather than flatten them into either `Mango denied safety value` or `Mango supported the rule`.

## Finding 6 — direct Slaoui evidence cuts against Mango's strongest retrospective characterization

On 2020-11-22, Slaoui was asked about Trump's allegation that Pfizer delayed good news until after the election. Slaoui said he lacked evidence of a deliberate delay and expressly characterized the **60-day follow-up as an appropriate decision** for understanding short-term and predictable longer-term safety.

Therefore:
- Mango's second-hand claim that Slaoui supplied a 95/99 calculation is not equivalent to Slaoui endorsing removal of the 60-day rule.
- Direct Slaoui evidence supports 60 days as scientifically appropriate.

## Bounded conclusion

The evidence now supports a more precise formulation: `42 days` was a historical/scientific benchmark for when most vaccine adverse events become apparent, while FDA's October `60-day / two-month median` rule was an added EUA evidentiary margin with an explicitly articulated scientific rationale. A fixed prior FDA COVID EUA rule of exactly 42 days has not been found. Direct statements from Slaoui and FDA vaccine scientists support two months as scientifically appropriate, while Mango's retrospective record both criticizes the incremental requirement and cites it as proof that COVID-vaccine safety review was more stringent than normal.
