## Purpose

Test whether the observed clustering of agency, litigation, personnel, and budget outcomes is exceptional after accounting for administration change, broad policy shifts, ordinary settlement behavior, agency-wide workforce reductions, and comparable conflict-management practices.

## Required negative controls

Negative controls should include matters satisfying the same administration, agency, period, and policy-change conditions but lacking:

- a beneficiary serving in government;
- a beneficiary with direct access to executive decision-makers;
- an active matter involving the beneficiary's companies;
- a personnel or structural change affecting the investigating body.

## Required positive controls

Positive controls should include historical cases where:

- an official held a regulated-company interest;
- recusal or waiver instruments were publicly documented;
- an official participated in a particular matter despite a financial interest;
- enforcement bodies were reorganized while matters affecting the official remained pending.

## Interpretation rules

1. Temporal clustering is a signal for investigation, not proof of causation.
2. A broad policy rationale is not automatically exculpatory; it must explain similarly situated non-beneficiary cases.
3. A favorable outcome is not evidence of improper influence without authority and participation evidence.
4. Absence of a located precedent is not proof that no precedent exists.
5. Requested, enacted, obligated, and expended budget values must remain separate.
6. Agency staffing change must be connected to matter-level consequences before causal weight is assigned.
7. A waiver, if located, must be tested for scope, timing, issuing authority, and the particular matters covered.
