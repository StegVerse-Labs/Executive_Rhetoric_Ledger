## Current bounded record

Observed/publicly reported dispute elements to reconstruct with primary-source custody:

- Article I, Section 4 Elections Clause assigns state legislatures the initial role over the Times, Places and Manner of congressional elections, subject to alteration by Congress.
- The executive branch has asserted authority through executive action and federal agencies, including USPS, touching voter-list / ballot-mail administration.
- The administration theory must be separated into presidential direction, agency statutory authority, and federal-law enforcement rationale rather than collapsed into a claim that the President directly controls elections.
- Litigation has challenged whether USPS implementation exceeds statutory or constitutional bounds.
- Political commentary, including Jamelle Bouie's August 29, 2026 New York Times opinion, characterizes the dispute as part of a broader expansion of executive power; that characterization is not treated as a neutral finding of fact.

## Initial hypotheses / competing explanations

H1: executive agencies are acting within congressionally delegated administrative authority and are not independently regulating elections.

H2: the executive branch is using federal infrastructure/statutory authorities in a manner that functionally imposes election-administration conditions beyond delegated authority.

H3: some challenged actions are lawful agency administration while particular implementation details exceed statutory authority; the dispute is severable rather than all-or-nothing.

H4: portions of the controversy are not yet merits-ripe or have been resolved on procedural grounds, leaving the constitutional boundary unsettled.
