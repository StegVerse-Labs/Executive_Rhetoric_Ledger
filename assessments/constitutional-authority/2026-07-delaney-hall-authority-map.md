# Constitutional Authority Map: Delaney Hall Protest and Use of Force

## Purpose

This map prevents tactical explanations from being treated as self-validating. Each exercise of coercive power must be connected to an asserted authority, the applicable legal burden, the evidence needed to establish that burden, and the consequence when the evidence is missing.

## Authority map

| Transition | Government action | Governing authority or constraint | Government must establish | Missing evidence consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Initial immigration arrest | Immigration statutes, administrative-warrant rules, Fourth and Fifth Amendment constraints | Identity, lawful arrest authority, factual basis, warrant or applicable exception, and accurate person matching | Arrest standing remains unverified; later custody cannot cure an unsupported initial seizure. |
| T2 | Continued civil detention | Immigration detention statutes and Fifth Amendment due process | Statutory detention category, custody-review posture, timely process, accurate records, and lawful continuation | Continued confinement legitimacy remains unestablished. |
| T3 | Access to counsel and proceedings | Fifth Amendment procedural due process and applicable immigration statutes/regulations | Notice, meaningful opportunity to be heard, language access, access to retained counsel, evidence access, and a review path capable of remedy | Procedure cannot be treated as meaningful merely because a docket or form exists. |
| T4 | Transfer of a detainee | Custody authority, anti-retaliation constraints, continuity of counsel and review | Lawful purpose, destination, medical and legal continuity, notice where required, and absence of retaliation for strike activity or organizing | Transfer may not be treated as an ordinary logistics action. |
| T5 | Removal or deportation | Final order or other lawful removal authority; Fifth Amendment process | Correct identity, executable order, exhaustion or status of review, no controlling stay, and opportunity to raise available claims | Physical removability does not establish legal removability. |
| T6 | Facility conditions and strike response | Fifth Amendment constraints on civil detention, statutory and regulatory detention standards, medical duties | Adequate food, water, sanitation, medical care, safety, grievance access, strike monitoring, and no unlawful retaliation | Official assurances do not satisfy the burden without inspectable records and independent access. |
| T7 | Restriction of protest | First Amendment speech, assembly, press, petition, and public-forum doctrine | Content-neutral lawful basis, narrow tailoring, adequate alternatives, clear notice, and non-retaliatory application | The protest's political viewpoint cannot supply the restriction rationale. |
| T8 | Dispersal order | First Amendment constraints and lawful crowd-control authority | Authorized issuer, objective safety basis, audible and specific order, feasible exit path, reasonable compliance time, and individualized enforcement | Presence after an inaudible or impossible-to-follow order does not establish resistance. |
| T9 | Arrest of a protester | Fourth Amendment reasonableness and probable-cause requirements | Individualized offense facts, identity of arresting personnel, time, location, warning history, and arrest record | Crowd proximity or shared purpose cannot substitute for individualized cause. |
| T10 | Physical or chemical force | Fourth Amendment reasonableness; component force policy; de-escalation and medical obligations | Immediate facts, severity, threat, active resistance, feasible alternatives, proportional level, stop point, intervention, and medical response | Force legitimacy remains not established; missing footage is a collection trigger, not presumptive justification. |
| T11 | Force against press, observers, officials, or mediators | First and Fourth Amendments and role-specific protections | Individualized threat or offense unrelated to protected observation, reporting, oversight, or mediation | Force cannot inherit justification from misconduct attributed to other people. |
| T12 | Post-event accountability | Agency policy, constitutional remedy structures, records and oversight law | Preserved video, reports, identities, complaint channels, independent review, corrective action, and disclosure posture | Inability or refusal to reconstruct weighs against claims of governed good-faith execution. |

## Linked-layer rule

The transitions are connected. A narrow operational rationale at T8, T9, or T10 does not automatically validate T1 through T7.

Examples:

- A person may be physically obstructing a vehicle, but the vehicle's proposed transfer still requires a lawful and non-retaliatory purpose.
- Officers may need to prevent immediate injury, but that does not establish that the detention, transfer, or removal being protected is lawful.
- A lawful detention operation would not authorize indiscriminate force against peaceful observers.
- An unlawful or unverified underlying operation does not automatically make every safety intervention unlawful, but it raises the evidence required before broader legitimacy is assigned.

## Evidence asymmetry rule

```text
The government possesses or controls much of the evidence necessary to justify detention, transfer, removal, arrest, and force.
Where those records are missing, withheld, destroyed, or non-reconstructable, the gap must not be charged against the person subjected to state power.
```

## Good-faith indicators

Evidence supporting governed good faith would include:

- identifiable and authorized personnel;
- preserved and reviewable orders, warrants, case status, and transfer records;
- prompt access to counsel and interpreters;
- independent inspection and medical access;
- clear warnings and feasible avenues for compliance;
- differentiation among peaceful protesters, observers, journalists, officials, and persons presenting an immediate threat;
- documented de-escalation attempts;
- force ending when the need ends;
- medical aid and reporting;
- independent review with authority to correct or remedy violations.

## Adverse indicators requiring heightened review

- masked or unidentifiable personnel without reconstructable role records;
- shifting or generalized official explanations;
- denied or materially restricted inspection;
- retaliation allegations linked to hunger-strike organizing or translation;
- rapid transfers that disrupt counsel, review, or medical continuity;
- chemical force against persons not individually shown to pose a threat;
- force after control is obtained;
- crowd-wide labels such as `rioters` used in place of individualized evidence;
- missing video, reports, warnings, or medical documentation;
- no independent process capable of remedy.

## Activation threshold

No force event should be promoted from `catalogued` to `justified`, `unjustified`, or `constitutional violation established` until the relevant transition row has:

1. an identified authority source;
2. an event-specific evidence packet;
3. contradictory evidence preserved;
4. missing evidence explicitly recorded;
5. an individualized necessity and proportionality assessment;
6. reviewer sign-off under the repository's dispute and deprecation policy.
