## ERL purpose

Preserve and analyze the article as a case study in epistemic transition governance: how verified technical developments, legitimate security risk, inference, hypothesis, prediction, and unsupported operational claims can become rhetorically compressed into a single authoritative-sounding narrative.

This record does **not** treat the article as proof that RSA-2048, ECC, or Western encrypted traffic has already been operationally broken by a Chinese quantum system.

## Claim-state separation

### A. Verified or independently verifiable technical developments

The following propositions are suitable for primary-source verification and may be admitted as observed facts only after source custody/reconstruction:

1. Jiuzhang 4.0 is a real photonic quantum-computing research system with reported quantum computational advantage on Gaussian boson sampling.
2. DeepSeek-V4 is a real contemporary AI model family/release line and is relevant to China’s domestic AI/compute capability trajectory.
3. “Harvest now, decrypt later” is a recognized cryptographic risk model.
4. Post-quantum cryptography standards and migration guidance already exist, including finalized NIST standards.

### B. Reasonable risk propositions

The following are legitimate risk hypotheses but are not equivalent to present compromise findings:

1. Cryptographically relevant quantum computing may arrive sooner than conservative forecasts.
2. Long-lived encrypted data collected today may become readable in the future if adversaries retain ciphertext until cryptographically relevant quantum capability becomes available.
3. Systems with long confidentiality horizons should adopt cryptographic agility and post-quantum migration before an operational quantum break is publicly demonstrated.

### C. Unsupported or insufficiently evidenced extrapolations

The following propositions must remain hypotheses unless independently reconstructed from authoritative evidence:

1. Jiuzhang 4.0’s Gaussian-boson-sampling advantage directly translates into arbitrary computational acceleration.
2. Jiuzhang 4.0 or another publicly demonstrated Chinese system can presently execute cryptographically relevant Shor-scale attacks against RSA-2048 or deployed ECC.
3. China currently possesses a classified operational quantum computer capable of decrypting RSA-2048/ECC at useful scale.
4. Western encrypted communications are presently being decrypted through such a system.
5. A Gaussian-boson-sampling speed comparison to a classical supercomputer can be treated as a direct cryptanalytic performance benchmark.

## Governed transition model

This case should be evaluated as a sequence of distinct epistemic states:

`OBSERVATION -> INFERENCE -> RISK HYPOTHESIS -> POLICY/ENGINEERING CONSEQUENCE`

A valid example:

`Observed quantum advantage on a bounded problem -> evidence of advanced national quantum capability -> nonzero risk of earlier-than-expected cryptographically relevant quantum computing -> accelerate PQC migration and cryptographic agility.`

An invalid or currently unsupported compression:

`Observed Gaussian boson sampling advantage -> therefore RSA-2048/ECC is already operationally broken -> therefore current intercepted traffic is being decrypted.`

ERL should preserve each transition independently and require evidence appropriate to the resulting claim state.

## StegVerse relevance

This candidate is directly relevant to StegVerse governance design because it demonstrates why authority should not be inferred from rhetorical confidence or technical adjacency. Governance should preserve provenance and admissibility across transitions among:

- observation;
- evidence;
- inference;
- hypothesis;
- prediction;
- consequence.

For cryptography specifically, the durable architectural objective is not merely “use a post-quantum algorithm.” It is to govern cryptographic transition state: algorithm admissibility, coexistence, migration, downgrade resistance, retirement, provenance, compatibility, evidence, and reconstruction.
