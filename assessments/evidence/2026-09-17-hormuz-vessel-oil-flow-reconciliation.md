## What can presently be said

1. **Physical transit exists.** The sources agree that vessels continue to move through Hormuz.
2. **Raw AIS visibility is not total traffic.** Both Reuters/Kpler reporting and Lloyd's List Intelligence explicitly preserve uncertainty from dark/off-AIS passages and retrospective identification.
3. **Independent reconstructed traffic remains below ordinary pre-conflict activity under the providers' own baselines.** Lloyd's describes current activity as far below pre-conflict levels even after dark-transit reconstruction.
4. **The U.S. operational count and public trackers are not an apples-to-apples pair.** U.S.-facilitated/protected ships, Lloyd's non-Iranian-linked cargo vessels, Kpler commodity vessels, and PortWatch AIS-derived all-vessel completed transits have different inclusion rules and revision behavior.
5. **Oil-volume estimates remain materially divergent.** Reuters reported an official figure above 17 million barrels/day against Kpler estimates around 4.3–5 million barrels/day during the same broader period of tracking opacity. The discrepancy is not resolved merely by acknowledging dark traffic.
6. **The White House cumulative 1,500-vessel / 750-million-barrel figures do not disclose enough time-window detail in the release to convert them safely into a daily average.** ERL therefore does not infer a daily rate from those cumulative numbers.
