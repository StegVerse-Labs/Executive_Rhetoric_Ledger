## Primary procurement rationale
The publicly posted redacted justification states that USDA selected Dynamic Aviation because it already had current experience with:
1. aerial dispersal of sterile New World screwworm flies in Panama and Mexico; and
2. active aerial-release contracts for sterile fruit flies in Florida and California.

USDA states that no sources-sought notice was posted before award and that no additional sources were planned to be solicited because of the urgency. Limited market research relied on required specifications, historical procurement experience, known licensed/capable aerial-dispersal companies, and current NWS aerial-release contracts.

USDA states pricing was determined fair and reasonable through historical dispersal-contract pricing and an Independent Government Cost Estimate using then-current market rates.

The justification explicitly commits USDA to advertise and compete one or more follow-on NWS aerial-dispersal contracts during the initial 6–12 months.

## Approval chain visible in public justification
- requirements certification: Matthew Walz, Emergency Aviation Specialist, signed June 8, 2026.
- Contracting Officer certification: Craig Harper, signed June 11, 2026.
- Competition Advocate approval: Craig Harper, signed June 11, 2026.
- Head of Contracting Activity approval: Craig Harper, signed June 11, 2026.

The public document therefore shows the same named official signing in three procurement capacities. For a contract above $20M but not exceeding $90M, current FAR 6.304 requires approval by the head of the procuring activity or a qualifying designee. This trace does not establish that the multiple-capacity signatures violated FAR or USDA procedures. It does create a governance question about role concentration and independent review that should be tested against USDA delegations and internal procurement policy.

## Market alternatives
Public federal award records show that Dynamic Aviation is not the only contractor used by APHIS for sterile-insect aerial-release work. Other firms, including Aerodescargas Fronterizas and Gulf Aviation, appear in related sterile-fruit-fly aerial-dispersal awards. This does not show that those firms could satisfy the NWS emergency requirement at the required scale, certifications, geography, aircraft configuration, or timing. It means the statement that no other source was practically available should be tested against capability rather than assumed from the one-offer result.

## Lobbying trace
Dynamic Aviation had an established federal lobbying relationship before the NWS award. A Q1 2025 federal disclosure reports $40,000 in lobbying income to PRASAM for Dynamic Aviation. The disclosed issues were defense/intelligence appropriations and ISR platforms/sensors, not USDA, screwworm, animal health, or this procurement.

Public lobbying indexes report Dynamic Aviation also paid PRASAM about $40,000 in Q1 2026 and $40,000 in Q2 2026. A second lobbying firm, Nestpoint Associates, registered Dynamic Aviation as a client in June 2026; the initial registration did not identify specific legislation and was filed after the June 12 NWS contract award.

Accordingly:
- lobbying relationship: verified;
- lobbying before award: verified;
- evidence that disclosed lobbying targeted this NWS procurement: not found in this pass;
- evidence that the post-award Nestpoint registration caused or influenced the already-made award: chronologically unsupported;
- improper influence finding: not authorized.

## Political-contribution context
Public Virginia campaign-finance records show Dynamic Aviation made a $25,000 contribution to Spirit of Virginia in October 2023 and provided in-kind aviation services to the same committee in 2023 and 2024. These records predate the NWS contract and are state-level political activity. No causal connection to the USDA award is established.

## Strong discriminator / next tests
1. Obtain USDA designation/delegation records showing Craig Harper's authority to act as Contracting Officer, Competition Advocate, and HCA for this procurement.
2. Determine whether USDA procurement policy permits or discourages the same official from performing all three roles on an urgent sole-source action of this value.
3. Obtain the Independent Government Cost Estimate and historical-pricing comparison, subject to lawful disclosure limits.
4. Reconstruct all known aerial-dispersal contractors and their NWS-relevant capabilities as of June 2026.
5. Track USDA's explicit promise to compete follow-on NWS aerial-dispersal work within 6–12 months; failure to do so would materially change procurement-risk posture.
6. Retrieve Q1/Q2 2026 PRASAM filings and the Nestpoint registration/report at source level to determine whether USDA, APHIS, screwworm, agriculture appropriations, or related executive-branch contacts appear.
