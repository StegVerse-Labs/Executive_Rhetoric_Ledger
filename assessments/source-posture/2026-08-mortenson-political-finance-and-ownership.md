# Mortenson Political-Finance and Ownership Source Posture

Status: `research_candidate`
As-of: 2026-08-22
Procurement-influence finding authorized: false
Trump-affiliation finding authorized: false

## Purpose
Test whether the Mortenson construction beneficiary associated with the Moore Air Base New World Screwworm facility, its controlling ownership, or identifiable senior family leadership has a documented campaign-finance relationship to Donald Trump or Trump-authorized federal campaign committees.

This record separates: (1) company ownership/control, (2) individual political contributions, (3) Trump-specific contribution evidence, and (4) any procurement-influence hypothesis.

## Ownership and leadership anchor
Mortenson publicly describes itself as a Minnesota-based family-owned construction, development, and engineering company. Its current leadership materials identify David Mortenson as Chairman and as President/CEO of parent company M. A. Mortenson Companies, Inc.; Mortenson also states that Derek Cunz became President and CEO of M. A. Mortenson Company on 2025-01-01, while David Mortenson remains Chairman. Mortenson Properties describes itself as the real-estate investment and ownership arm of the Mortenson family portfolio of companies.

These records support treating David Mortenson as a high-value controlling-family/ownership subject for political-finance screening. They do not establish that every family member is an owner of the construction entity or that any individual contribution is attributable to the company.

## Verified federal contribution records
### David Mortenson
FEC filings identify David Mortenson with employer `M.A. Mortenson Company` or `Mortenson Construction` and occupation `Chairman`.

Verified examples located in the current sweep include:
- 2025-03-28: $3,500 contribution earmarked through ActBlue to `Angie Craig for Congress`; the filing reports $7,000 election-cycle-to-date for David Mortenson.
- 2025-10-28: $3,500 contribution earmarked through ActBlue to `Matt Klein for Congress`.
- 2026-03-11: $3,500 contribution earmarked through ActBlue to `Morrison for Congress`; the filing reports $7,000 election-cycle-to-date.

The use of ActBlue is a conduit fact, not itself proof of the recipient candidate's ideology. The underlying FEC committee records identify the recipient committees named above. These transactions are evidence of federal political giving by the Mortenson chairman, not of corporate contributions by M. A. Mortenson Company.

### Alice D. Mortenson
FEC filings identify Alice D. Mortenson with employer `Mortenson Family Foundation` and occupation `President`.

Verified example:
- 2025-11-11: two $3,500 receipts, one designated primary and one general, reported by `McBride for Delaware Inc.`, for a $7,000 election-cycle-to-date total.

This is evidence of political giving by a Mortenson-family foundation leader. It does not by itself establish ownership/control of M. A. Mortenson Company, and the charitable foundation is analytically distinct from the construction company.

## Trump-specific search result
The current search checked Trump candidate/committee records and broad FEC-indexed references for `Mortenson`, `M.A. Mortenson`, David Mortenson, Alice Mortenson, Derek Cunz, Dan Johnson, and Jack Mortenson against Trump-related contribution terms.

No verified contribution by M. A. Mortenson Company, David Mortenson, or Alice D. Mortenson to `Donald J. Trump for President 2024, Inc.` or another Trump-authorized committee was located in this pass.

This is a negative-search result, not proof of absence. A complete determination requires direct FEC transaction-level export/query across aliases, addresses, employer variants, joint-fundraising committees, leadership PACs, party committees, super PACs, spouses/related family members, and records in which employer fields are missing or inconsistent.

## Procurement interpretation boundary
- A political contribution by an owner, executive, employee, or family member does not establish procurement influence.
- A contribution to a candidate opposing or unrelated to Trump does not prove political opposition by the company as a whole.
- No Trump-affiliation, favoritism, quid pro quo, steering, or corruption finding is authorized from the evidence currently located.
- The strongest verified owner-level evidence presently located points to federal contributions routed through ActBlue to non-Trump candidate committees, which weakens—but does not conclusively eliminate—the hypothesis that the Moore Air Base award is readily explained by a direct Trump-campaign donor relationship.
- The Moore Air Base procurement must still be evaluated on competition, evaluation criteria, price reasonableness, modifications, subcontracting, USACE/USDA governance, and award-level accounting independent of political-finance findings.
