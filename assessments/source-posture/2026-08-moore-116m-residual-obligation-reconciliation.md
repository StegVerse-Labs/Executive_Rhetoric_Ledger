# Moore Air Base $116.406M Residual Obligation Reconciliation

Status: `research_candidate`
As-of: 2026-08-22
Corruption/waste finding authorized: false
Accounting reconciliation complete: false

## Question
What accounts for the $116,405,969 difference between CRS's FY2026-through-May `Moore Air Base Production Facility Construction` obligation of $726,405,969 and the fully obligated $610,000,000 Mortenson prime contract `W9126G-26-C-A011`?

## Verified arithmetic
- CRS reports $726,405,969 obligated to `Moore Air Base Production Facility Construction` through May 2026.
- Federal contract-announcement reporting identifies M. A. Mortenson Co. contract `W9126G-26-C-A011` as a $610,000,000 firm-fixed-price award on 2026-03-06.
- The same award announcement states $610,000,000 in FY2026 USDA funds was obligated at award and that two bids were received.
- Residual outside the prime-award amount: `$726,405,969 - $610,000,000 = $116,405,969`.

The residual is not an unfunded portion of the Mortenson award because the full prime award amount was obligated at award.

## Scope controls
### Separate Moore dispersal-center project
CRS separately reports `Moore Air Base Dispersal Center Project` obligations of $9,053,469 across FY2025-FY2026. Public Texas animal-health meeting material describes the completed domestic dispersal facility at approximately $8.5M. Because CRS assigns this activity to its own line item, the dispersal-center project should not be used to explain the $116.406M production-facility residual absent contrary accounting evidence.

### Specialized production equipment
Public acquisition summaries for solicitation `W9126G26RA033` describe specialized irradiation and diet-mixing equipment and state that some equipment may be installed by directed subcontractors. This demonstrates that highly specialized equipment is contemplated in the production-facility delivery model, but it does not establish whether any such equipment is inside the $610M firm-fixed-price prime amount or separately obligated. No residual allocation is made without an award/instrument record.

## Interagency-accounting hypothesis
USACE Fort Worth District is USDA's design and construction agent for the current production facility. USDA has historical precedent at Moore Air Base for using an interagency agreement with the Army Corps of Engineers to manage construction: USDA's FY2019 APHIS explanatory notes state that APHIS engaged ACOE through an interagency agreement for the prior Mexfly rearing-facility replacement and spent $111,985 in FY2017 on the interagency agreement and a program-of-requirements contract.

This historical precedent makes a USDA-to-USACE reimbursable/interagency funding layer a concrete hypothesis for part of the current residual. It does **not** establish that the 2026 $116.406M residual is a USACE fee, reserve, contingency, design cost, or other specific category.

## Acquisition targets
1. USDA-APHIS to USACE interagency agreement, reimbursable work authorization, funding document, and amendments for the production facility.
2. USACE project-accounting/work-breakdown ledger showing total reimbursable authority, obligations, commitments, fees, contingency, and downstream awards.
3. Complete CLIN schedule and statement of work for `W9126G-26-C-A011` to determine which specialized equipment/design/commissioning elements are inside the $610M FFP amount.
4. All separate architect-engineer, construction-management, enabling-work, utility/site, commissioning, validation, and equipment awards charged to the production-facility project.
5. Source-selection and two-step acquisition records for `W9126G26RA033`, including qualification-stage pool, final two bidders, evaluation criteria, and individual subcontracting plan.

## Promotion boundary
The existence of a $116,405,969 residual does not establish waste, overpayment, hidden contractor profit, improper steering, or an unexplained cash expenditure. The current evidence establishes only an unreconciled obligation difference between a USDA/APHIS program category and a downstream $610M prime contract.
