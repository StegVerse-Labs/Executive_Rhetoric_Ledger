### Interpretation boundary
- These are obligations under USDA emergency-transfer authorities, not necessarily cash expenditures completed by the report date.
- The $726.4M Moore Air Base construction obligation is larger than the publicly announced $610M Mortenson design-build contract value. The difference is not yet reconciled and may include government-furnished work, design/support, contingency, site/infrastructure, other contracts, or accounting scope differences. No conclusion is authorized until award-level reconciliation is completed.
- The $999.2M total predates the June 2026 public Grand Challenge award announcement and June 2026 Dynamic Aviation contract, so headline post-June totals cannot simply be added without checking whether later awards draw from already-obligated emergency-transfer balances.

## Moore Air Base ownership and project-delivery clarification
The current Moore Air Base project should not be treated as construction on an active Air Force installation or as a private-site acquisition. USDA Agricultural Research Service records identify the relevant location as `APHIS-VS property known as Moore Air Base` and describe the Cattle Fever Tick Research Laboratory as operating on 222.943 acres of APHIS property in Edinburg, Texas. Historical sources trace the installation from its former Air Force use into USDA agricultural use; the precise historical disposal date is secondary to the present ownership/control fact for this cost-reconciliation question.

USACE states that its Fort Worth District serves as the design and construction agent for the NWS production-facility project. USDA/APHIS announced the Mortenson construction contract, and USACE describes the new facility as being constructed by Mortenson at a total project cost of $610M.

### Procurement implication
Because the relevant site is already USDA/APHIS-controlled federal property, the unresolved difference between the CRS `Moore Air Base Production Facility Construction` obligation of $726,405,969 and the public $610M project/contract headline should not be provisionally explained as purchase of the Moore site. Reconciliation should instead seek the complete set of construction-program obligations: prime contract and modifications, USACE design/construction-agent costs, enabling/site/infrastructure work, government-furnished equipment, separate engineering/support contracts, contingency or reserve treatment, commissioning/validation, and any other obligations booked to the CRS construction category.

This clarification does **not** establish waste, overpayment, improper profit, or procurement misconduct. It narrows the accounting question and removes an unsupported land-acquisition explanation from the default hypothesis set.

## Standing annual NWS baseline
CRS reports APHIS used approximately $20M per fiscal year since FY2012 for production of about 20M sterile insects per week and field activity in the Panama barrier zone. Before FY2012, the annual amount was approximately $28M while two production facilities were supported for part of that period.

This creates a better denominator for cost comparison: the emergency response is not being compared to zero prior spending; the United States maintained a recurring NWS containment program before the current emergency.

## Breakdown-control evidence
CRS states that neither USDA, foreign governments, nor WOAH had publicly determined why the Panama biological barrier failed. USDA identified possible contributing conditions already present before the current administration, including increased cattle population in Darién, producer wound-care/education issues, and reduced supply/quality of inputs needed for sterile-fly production.

Therefore, the barrier failure cannot be attributed to 2025 policy changes. The valid current-administration question concerns how 2025-2026 policy changed resilience, detection, response margin, cattle supply, and cost after the northward outbreak was already underway.

## U.S. return and trade effects
- First current-outbreak U.S. detection: 2026-06-03, a calf in Zavala County, Texas.
- CRS reports 45 confirmed U.S. detections as of 2026-08-10: 44 in Texas and 1 in New Mexico.
- Canada subsequently restricted imports of susceptible animals from Texas; USDA export guidance requires additional NWS certification for animals exported from other states and excludes animals that resided in or transited Texas during the prior 21 days.
- USDA announced phased reopening of Mexican livestock imports. APHIS currently lists Douglas, Arizona for reopening on 2026-08-24; Santa Teresa and Columbus, New Mexico remain to be determined.

## Cattle-supply control
CRS reports Mexico supplied more than 60% of U.S. cattle imports in 2023 and 2024, approximately 1.25M head annually, while the domestic herd was near historical lows. This supports a measurable supply-channel effect from prolonged import restriction, while preserving the countervailing biosecurity rationale.

## New unresolved questions
1. Reconcile the CRS $726.406M Moore Air Base production-facility obligation with the $610M Mortenson/project value, excluding unsupported assumptions that the difference represents acquisition of the already USDA/APHIS-controlled Moore site.
2. Obtain award-level components for the Moore construction category, including prime award/modifications, USACE agent costs, enabling/site work, government-furnished equipment, engineering/support, commissioning, contingency/reserve treatment, and separate contracts.
3. Determine whether June 2026 Grand Challenge agreements and Dynamic Aviation obligations are included in, additional to, or funded from the already obligated emergency-transfer totals.
4. Obtain award-level components underlying `International Outreach, Surveillance, and Control` ($47.363M) and compare their functions/geography with foreign-assistance animal-health capacity that was separately reduced or terminated.
5. Quantify Canadian trade restriction impact and any additional state/interstate movement costs caused by U.S. detections.
6. Track actual Mexican cattle entries after the scheduled Douglas reopening and any suspension/reversal tied to surveillance results.
