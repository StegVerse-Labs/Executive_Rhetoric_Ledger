# Source Posture — Foreign Animal-Health Capacity Cuts and Partial Restoration

Status: `research_candidate_support`
Administration-caused-outbreak finding authorized: `false`
Risk-amplification finding authorized: `bounded`

## Core evidence

### FAO / Emergency Centre for Transboundary Animal Diseases (ECTAD)
Multiple public sources converge on a major 2025 loss of U.S. support for FAO animal-disease work:
- reporting on the list of terminated USAID awards sent to Congress identified about $250M in FAO Global Health Security projects among the terminations;
- affected work included animal-disease monitoring and containment, including avian influenza, New World Screwworm in Central America, and swine fever;
- FAO leadership later stated that the United States had historically supplied about 90% of ECTAD funding and that the loss of roughly $60M/year reduced technical prevention, control, surveillance, laboratory, and rapid-response capacity to near termination;
- FAO later confirmed that some planned country support for disease surveillance, laboratory strengthening, veterinary training, and outbreak preparedness could not be implemented as planned or had to be delayed or scaled back.

### Scale/function of the standing capability
The ECTAD/global-health-security system is relevant to U.S. exposure risk because it supports:
- early warning and field surveillance;
- veterinary laboratory networks and diagnostics;
- trained animal-health workforces;
- outbreak investigation and response;
- cross-border information sharing and risk communication;
- support for national veterinary services in countries where transboundary diseases emerge or move.

A 2026 Senate Foreign Relations minority letter states that FAO's transboundary-animal-disease work established early warning and surveillance systems in more than 130 countries and assisted in more than 250 outbreak responses involving avian influenza, African swine fever, foot-and-mouth disease, and other threats. This is a congressional characterization and should be independently checked against FAO program records before publication as an adjudicated ERL fact.

### Feed the Future Innovation Labs
Congressional and agricultural-sector sources also identify roughly $72M associated with 16 Feed the Future Innovation Labs that lost funding/operations in 2025. The labs were not all dedicated to animal disease, so they must not be treated as a $72M animal-health cut. Some supported livestock management, veterinary vaccines, food safety, and agricultural research that can contribute indirectly to resilience against transboundary disease.

Congress later appropriated/restored $72M for FY2026 Innovation Lab funding, but subsequent reporting indicates the replacement portfolio may reopen fewer labs under new competitions rather than simply restoring all 16 prior capabilities. Restoration of headline dollars therefore does not yet establish restoration of personnel, institutional relationships, geography, or technical function.

## Important counterevidence / causal boundary
- New World Screwworm had already escaped the historical Panama barrier and was moving north before January 2025.
- Experts cited in independent fact checking have stated that inadequate sterile-fly production was a pre-existing constraint and that once NWS reached Nicaragua, continued northward spread may have been difficult or impossible to stop with existing capacity.
- Therefore, the 2025 foreign-assistance cuts cannot presently be characterized as the cause of the NWS resurgence or U.S. detections.
- The stronger supported proposition is that the cuts reduced surveillance, laboratory, veterinary-training, and preparedness capacity during an active transboundary-animal-health threat, decreasing early-warning/response margin and potentially increasing exposure risk.

## Partial restoration / replacement
Evidence indicates later U.S. and multilateral actions rebuilt or added some capabilities:
- USDA maintained and expanded NWS-specific cooperation with Mexico, COPEG, IICA, OIRSA, and other partners;
- Congress restored FY2026 funding for Feed the Future Innovation Labs, although implementation differs from the prior portfolio;
- State Department successor global-health programs have begun rebuilding selected One Health / global-health-security capacity in countries including Guatemala;
- FAO/IAEA launched a new five-year coordinated research project in June 2026 to improve NWS surveillance, mass rearing, sterilization, and release methods across more than 20 affected countries.

These later actions reduce the accuracy of any claim that international animal-health capacity was simply 'eliminated and never restored.' The research question is instead how much capability was lost, for how long, at which locations, and at what cost to reconstruct.

## Emerging cost-comparison frame
Do not compare $250M of terminated multi-country awards directly with the $610M Moore Air Base facility or the $105M Grand Challenge as if they are functionally equivalent. The correct comparison is function-by-function:

| Function | Pre-cut standing capability | 2025 loss/disruption | 2025-26 replacement/augmentation | Current status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| early warning / field surveillance | FAO/ECTAD + national veterinary partners | multiple projects terminated or scaled back | USDA bilateral work; selected State/FAO/IAEA rebuilding | partial / geography-specific |
| laboratories / diagnostics | ECTAD-supported networks | planned support delayed/scaled back | Grand Challenge diagnostics + bilateral capacity | partial |
| veterinary training | ECTAD / country support | reduced/delayed | IICA/USDA regional training; successor programs | partial |
| sterile-fly production | COPEG pre-existing, already insufficient for resurgence | not eliminated by aid cuts | Metapa/Mission/Moore expansion | major augmentation underway |
| aerial sterile-fly dispersal | existing specialist contractors | not shown eliminated | emergency Dynamic Aviation award + future competition promised | augmented |
