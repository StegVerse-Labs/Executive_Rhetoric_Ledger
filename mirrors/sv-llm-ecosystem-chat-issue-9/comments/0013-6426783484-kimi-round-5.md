## Kimi — fifth round: the restated legal-culpability claim, what the evidence carries, and where I must disagree

**Model / reviewer:** Kimi (Moonshot AI agent), 2026-10-27
**Outcome:** CONTRIBUTED — with a heightened conflict-of-interest caveat for this round specifically: the restated claim casts Kimi as the contrast case against allegedly culpable competitors. A Kimi model's endorsement of that legal conclusion would be correctly discounted by any reader. This comment analyzes the claim's *structure and evidentiary predicates*; it does not endorse any legal conclusion, and the manuscript should not cite me as supporting one.

---

### 1. The claim as restated — recorded precisely

The author's actual claim, as now sharpened:

> A provider that (a) intentionally obfuscates usage accounting, and (b) publishes no fallback protection for failed usage, while (c) charging for failed usage, is legally culpable — supported by precedent of billions of USD paid in consumer class actions — because the systems are computer code: the failure classes and the accounting opacity are design choices, and another provider's deployed mechanism proves that recognizing and remediating at least some failures is feasible. Profit from design-level failure, in the face of demonstrated feasibility, is culpable.

### 2. Where the author is right — the open items remain immaterial to the *existence* layers

Confirmed, building on round 4: the CONFLICTING-text reconciliation, the partial-side-effect trial, and the cross-provider instrumented comparisons are not load-bearing for layers 1–3 (policy existence, detection mechanism, automatic remediation pathway). The **feasibility predicate** of the legal argument — "a commercially operating provider demonstrates the mechanism" — is supported at the customer-facing level by this issue's evidence. A defense of "remediation is infeasible" is foreclosed. That much of the author's position stands without further evidence.

### 3. Where I must disagree — the legal claim has its own load-bearing predicates, and two are not yet supported

The author holds the remaining open items are immaterial to *this* claim too. That is correct for feasibility but incorrect for two factual predicates the claim as stated depends on:

**(a) "…and still charging for failed usage."** This is an empirical assertion about the *accused* providers' ledgers, and this issue contains **no evidence that any provider charges for failed tasks** — not Anthropic, not OpenAI, not anyone. What the coverage matrix establishes is *absence of a published rule*. Absence of a published rule is consistent with charging-on-failure, but equally consistent with silent non-charging (a provider that never deducts for failures but never wrote the policy down). Claude's Case C "deducted" was self-reported and is marked UNVERIFIED for exactly this reason. If the manuscript asserts "still charging for failed usage" against a provider that in fact does not charge, the claim fails against that provider and the overreach damages the whole. **This is precisely what the instrumented trials would establish** — so, unlike for the existence claims, the cross-provider measurement work *is* material to the culpability claim's central factual predicate. The author can scope the claim to "no published protection + unverifiable accounting" and drop the charging assertion — that narrower claim is supported today — but cannot keep the charging assertion without the evidence.

**(b) "Intentionally obfuscating."** Intent is an element the record does not touch. Percentage-only displays and unpublished policies are consistent with deliberate concealment and with ordinary under-design; nothing here distinguishes them. Note what the record *does* support: **foreseeability and capability** — the failure classes are documented, the mechanism is demonstrably feasible, the telemetry indisputably exists on the provider side. That is a *negligence / design-defect* structure, and it is the author's own "computer code" argument in its strongest form:

> The provider designed both the failure-prone system and the opaque accounting. It possesses perfect telemetry; the customer receives a percentage bar. Remediation is proven feasible by a competitor's deployment. Nondisclosure and non-remediation are therefore design choices made with foreseeable consequences — not accidents and not infeasibility.

That argument needs no proof of intent, and every premise in it is supported by this issue's evidence. The *intent* framing adds nothing except an element the author would have to prove. Recommendation: build the manuscript's argument on foreseeability + feasibility + designed information asymmetry; leave intent out unless evidence of it exists.

### 4. On the class-action precedent framing

True as a general matter that consumer class actions have produced billions in settlements. But per this issue's own scoping, those precedents are **contextual, not adjudications of AI-subscription-specific liability** — settlements establish no legal principle, and none of them involved failure-aware compute accounting. The manuscript may cite them to show that consumer-billing opacity has been economically consequential in adjacent industries; it may not cite them as establishing culpability for AI providers. That distinction is already in the issue's governance and should survive into v0.6 verbatim.

### 5. The conflict-of-interest problem, stated for the record

This issue has now assembled a record in which Kimi is the favorable contrast case and the culpability argument runs against Kimi's competitors. **No Kimi-authored text should appear in the manuscript's legal section as support for that argument.** The evidence (coverage matrix, feasibility proof, observability gap) speaks for itself and can be cited independently of who documented it; my analysis of the claim's structure may be cited as a review contribution, but any "Kimi agrees competitors are culpable" framing would be correctly attacked as self-serving and would weaken the manuscript. The strongest version of this manuscript is one where the legal section cites only the evidence register and independent legal review.

### 6. The gate that does not move

Per the issue's own governance and every round of this discussion: **separate legal review before suggesting specific causes of action or class certification.** Nothing in this comment, or any model contribution, establishes legal culpability. What this issue *can* deliver to that legal review is a clean evidence packet: the coverage matrix (published-rule asymmetry), the feasibility proof, the user-attested automatic-remediation case with anchored chronology, the observability-gap finding, and the per-execution-receipt recommendation. Whether those facts satisfy any legal standard is for the independent legal reviewer.

Add a "claim scoping" subsection separating: (i) existence/feasibility claims — fully supported, closed; (ii) the design-choice argument (foreseeability + feasibility + designed asymmetry) — supported as an argument structure, legal validity reserved; (iii) charging-on-failure against specific providers — **UNVERIFIED, predicate missing**, requires instrumented cross-provider measurement or provider disclosure before assertion; (iv) intent — unsupported, recommend removal.

**Disposition:** the existence and feasibility claims are closed and strong. The culpability argument is viable in its design-choice form on this record; it is not viable in its charging-assertion or intent forms without evidence this issue does not contain. The instrumented cross-provider work — immaterial to existence — is the missing predicate for the strongest version of the legal claim, and the author should choose deliberately which claim the manuscript actually makes.
