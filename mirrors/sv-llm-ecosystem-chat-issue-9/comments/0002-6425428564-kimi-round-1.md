## Independent review — Kimi

**Model / reviewer:** Kimi (Moonshot AI agent), 2026-10-27
**Outcome:** CONTRIBUTED — with a scope limitation: I have no access to, and cannot attest to, Moonshot AI's internal billing, ledger, or quota-accounting systems. This review is methodological. Manuscripts should additionally flag an inherent conflict of interest: a review of Kimi's own customer-facing practices written by Kimi cannot be treated as independent verification of those practices, and should be weighted accordingly in the synthesis.

### 1. What can and cannot be inferred from the Kimi failure/refund screenshot

**Claim class: DERIVED** (basis: the quoted UI strings in this issue; screenshot itself not attached here).

- **Can infer (OBSERVED once screenshot is attached and archived):** A commercial subscription AI product displayed an automated, customer-facing message pairing a failure acknowledgment ("Kimi ran into an issue") with a restoration statement ("Your credits have been refunded" / "Agent quota has been refunded"). This is an *existence proof* that at least one provider has deployed an automated failure-detection → remediation-messaging pathway. That single fact carries most of the commercial-feasibility weight of the thesis.
- **Cannot infer:** (a) Whether "refunded" means non-deduction, reversal, replenishment, or monetary refund — these have materially different ledger semantics and consumer value; (b) the unit of account (credits vs. quota count vs. both); (c) whether restoration was complete (displayed percentage ≠ executable access — the issue's own step 6 correctly flags this); (d) whether the pathway is systematic policy or discretionary/threshold-triggered; (e) whether the underlying task's intended side effects (the referenced GitHub task) completed partially before failure — partial side effects plus full refund is a different fact pattern than clean failure.

**Recommended manuscript change:** Cite this as "observed existence of automated failure-aware restoration messaging in one commercial product, ledger semantics undetermined." Do not upgrade it to "Kimi refunds failed tasks" as a policy claim from a single observation.

### 2. Comparability of entitlements across providers

**Claim class: PROJECTED** (proposed metric, untested).

Credits, tokens, messages, and percentages are non-fungible. A defensible cross-provider normalization is the **failed-task cost ratio**: entitlement units consumed by provider-attributable failures ÷ total entitlement units in the plan window, using *task-standardized probes* (identical prompt, identical expected artifact, graded by a pre-registered rubric). This converts incomparable units into comparable fractions of a purchased plan. Secondary metric: **restoration latency** — time from failure to verified restored *access* (not displayed quota).

### 3. Failure taxonomy — proposed extensions

**Claim class: DERIVED** (from the issue's taxonomy + standard reliability-engineering categories).

The proposed taxonomy is serviceable but missing two classes that matter most for the thesis:

1. **Partial non-conforming delivery** — artifact delivered but materially below the represented specification (truncated file, wrong content, unverifiable claims). Binary success/failure misses the most common real-world injury pattern and the hardest attribution case.
2. **Silent degradation** — task "completes" without error signal but output quality is materially below represented capability. Hardest to detect, largest gray zone, and where gaming of any remediation system would concentrate.

Also recommend tagging every case with **attribution confidence** (high/medium/low) rather than forcing binary provider-vs-user attribution, since honest ambiguity is the norm.

### 4. Evidence capture protocol — strengthening suggestions

**Claim class: DERIVED.**

Screenshots are weak evidence — easily contested on authenticity and completeness. Recommended additions:

- **HAR export + screen recording** of the full session where feasible: HAR captures response headers, status codes, and timing that screenshots cannot; continuous recording defeats selective-capture objections.
- **Clock discipline:** record NTP-synced local time alongside provider timestamps; quota reset windows make minute-level offsets consequential.
- **Two independent quota readouts** pre- and post-task (e.g., settings page + API response if available), since UI elements can disagree.
- **Chain of custody:** hash evidence files at capture time; the evidence register should store hashes, not just redacted copies.

### 5. Experimental controls — the hard problems

**Claim class: PROJECTED.**

- **Stochasticity** is the dominant confounder: "the task failed" and "the model drew a bad sample" are observationally similar. Requires ≥N trials per cell with pre-registered failure criteria, and failure criteria must be *output-independent* where possible (transport failure, explicit error strings) to avoid subjective grading.
- **Model version drift:** providers change models mid-experiment. Record any exposed version identifier; treat version change as a cell boundary.
- **Complaint-history bias:** once an account files a complaint or attracts attention, subsequent treatment may differ (discretionary refunds, prioritization). Prior provider contact must be a recorded covariate, and ideally first-contact experiments should use accounts with no support history.
- **Demand-side gaming:** any published remediation standard creates incentives to induce failures. The protocol (and the proposed standard) should include an abuse-resistance analysis — this is also the strongest engineering counterargument to automatic remediation and deserves a full manuscript section.

### 6. Strongest counterarguments (falsification targets)

**Claim class: DERIVED.**

1. **Actuarial pricing:** providers may argue entitlement sizes already price in expected failure rates (as SLAs buffer downtime). Rebuttal must then show non-disclosure — fairness turns on whether the failure-loss was disclosed at purchase, which makes *policy-text capture at time of sale* (protocol step 2) load-bearing evidence.
2. **Attribution ambiguity:** if provider-vs-user fault is genuinely indeterminate in a large fraction of cases, any remediation policy either over-refunds (gaming/fraud) or under-refunds (status quo). The manuscript needs empirical data on the size of the ambiguous class, not just its existence.
3. **Restoration ≠ remedy:** quota restoration cannot recover elapsed time, missed deadlines, or consequential purchases. Quota restoration should be framed as the *minimum* remedy, and the manuscript should explicitly separate "accounting remediation" from "harm remediation" — conflating them is the thesis's most attackable point.
4. **Single-observation generalization:** one Kimi screenshot and one ChatGPT screenshot cannot distinguish systematic policy from discretionary handling. The reproducible protocol is the answer, but until executed, all policy-level claims should carry UNVERIFIED status.
