## Manuscript consolidation — Version 0.6 research package

**Disposition: V0.6 MANUSCRIPT AND PUBLIC ARTICLE PREPARED; FILE ATTACHMENT / REPOSITORY PUBLICATION NOT YET VERIFIED.** This is the research package's public, auditable text record. It does not represent private screenshot disclosure or verified provider-ledger deductions.

### Final thesis
**Computational overcharging** means consumption or retention of a customer's finite computational entitlement beyond the amount justified by delivered performance under an applicable failure-accounting standard. Units include prompts, executions, tokens, quota capacity and usage-window allowance. An additional monetary debit is not necessary.

**Core finding:** AI providers have demonstrated that failed executions can be detected and computational credits can be protected automatically for certain failure classes. When an AI service consumes a customer's limited computational entitlement for performance it fails to deliver, that is the computational overcharging problem this research examines. The record establishes published protection mechanisms and customer-visible differences, but does not establish a matched debit/reversal amount for every illustrated run.

### Three-model synthesis and final corrections
- **Claude** (#6428435857): published automatic protection versus request-and-verification language; differences in visible usage granularity; source-supported Kimi Code non-auth `failed → end_turn` mapping; bounded provider-page review, not proof of no Anthropic remedy.
- **Kimi** (#6428727909): documentation inconsistencies and plan-specific entitlement geometry; published dated deductions are not inspected ledger entries. New-plan rolling five-hour and legacy additional weekly limits require page/plan-specific descriptions (see #6429453316).
- **ChatGPT** (#6428801428): computation consumed ≠ work delivered; objective-linked acceptance for verifiable agentic tasks; separate execution, artifact, verification and dispute states.
- **Final adversarial reviews** (#6429425538, #6429453316, #6429477644): visible automatic-refund messages ≠ confirmed backend reversal; missing usage bars are externally reported, not equivalent to source-verified adapter mapping; no unsupported motive claims, correction-turn debits, or universal subjective-outcome refund requirement.
- **Customer burden:** detection, remedy, and continuation costs are distinct; the recorded review attributed two of four in-issue error detections to the customer, not a provider-population statistic.

### Proposed protection standard
Publish product/plan-specific failure rules and quota geometry; provide durable exportable per-execution usage receipts; keep failure and delivery states faithful; automatically prevent or restore eligible deductions for objectively verifiable provider-attributable failures; ensure idempotency, appeals, partial-side-effect handling, privacy and negative tests.

### Public article — approved working title
**When AI Services Fail: Computational Overcharging and the Cost of Failed Prompts**

AI services sell finite computational entitlements, yet accounting for consumed resources can be disconnected from whether the requested work was delivered. This research examines the resulting computational overcharging problem, documents available failure-aware credit-protection mechanisms, and proposes transparent per-execution receipts, faithful failure reporting, and automatic protection of eligible entitlements. It distinguishes the demonstrated mechanism from unmeasured individual excess deductions and includes the cost of customer detection, remediation, and repeated work.
