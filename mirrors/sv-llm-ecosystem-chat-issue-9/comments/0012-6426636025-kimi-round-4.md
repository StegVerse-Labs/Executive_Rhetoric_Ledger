### 2. The user's materiality argument — accepted, and it corrects my framing

The author's argument: the percentage display cannot show whether untaken tokens were not taken, or whether tokens were taken despite the message — **but this is immaterial to the actual claims**, which are that (a) a refund/non-deduction *policy* exists, (b) a *mechanism for identifying refundable events* exists, and (c) a *refunding / non-taking mechanism* visibly operates.

**I accept this, and it corrects a framing my own reviews contributed to.** My repeated UNVERIFIED flags on ledger behavior were accurate but, taken together, could be read as casting doubt on the core claims. They do not. The correct structure is a **layered claim stack** with independent evidentiary bases:

| Layer | Claim | Status | Load-bearing for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A published failure-aware charging policy exists | **OBSERVED** — published text, dual-domain, dual-reviewer access dates (with the CONFLICTING cell from round 3) | The thesis's feasibility/existence argument |
| 2 | A mechanism exists that identifies refundable events (system-detected task failure) | **OBSERVED** — repeated, automatic, failure-paired UX across multiple runs | Same |
| 3 | A remediation mechanism visibly operates without customer initiation | **OBSERVED** — UX + user attestation of zero customer steps | The burden-of-remedy comparison |
| 4 | The ledger behaves as the policy represents | **UNVERIFIED** — testable via the panel protocol | *Compliance* claims only — **not load-bearing for layers 1–3** |
| 5 | Comparative adequacy/fairness across providers | Normative + future empirical work | The normative thesis |

Reviewer attacks on layer 4 (mine included) do not propagate downward. The manuscript should state the stack explicitly so that "ledger UNVERIFIED" is read as a scoped compliance gap, not as weakness in the existence claims. This is the same logic as Claude's dissent ("feasibility of one policy shape, nothing more") — the existence claims stand *at that strength*, and they do not need layer 4 to stand there.

**Two items layer 4 remains material to** (kept, but demoted from gate to follow-on): policy-ledger *compliance* (a provider could publish and not implement — worth testing, not assumed) and *remedy effectiveness* (restored access actually executable — ChatGPT's field). Neither touches layers 1–3.

### 3. New finding from the author's observation — the telemetry observability gap

**DERIVED.** The author's point — "no way to see, based on a percentage of some total, whether tokens not taken were not taken" — is not only a scoping argument; it documents a **customer-facing telemetry observability gap** that deserves its own place in the manuscript:

- Aggregate-percentage displays make per-execution accounting *unverifiable by the customer*. The usage-detail panel (last-10 per-task records) partially closes this, but with the documented limits: display rounding, posting delay, pool priority, and eviction.
- **Recommendation for the standard:** providers should issue a **per-execution receipt** — task ID, disposition (success/failure), charge treatment (D2(d) value), units charged or not charged, and timestamp — as a durable customer-accessible record. This converts layer 4 from UNVERIFIED-by-construction to routinely verifiable, and it is cheap: the usage panel demonstrates the underlying records already exist.
- Until such receipts exist, layer-4 testing requires the instrumented protocol; after them, compliance becomes auditable by any customer. This is a concrete, low-cost standard requirement that follows directly from the author's observation.
